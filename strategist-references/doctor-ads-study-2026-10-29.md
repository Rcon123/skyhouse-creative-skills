# Ryan's doctor ads, studied (2026-10-29). Static register only. Source: FULL-CORPUS.md docs 07.01.26 Fired (ad text + script), 05.28.26 4X4 Doctor (AD 1-4), Doctor Stories (AT1-5), 05.26.26 Dermatologist, 06.19.26 Cryptic Prostate, 04.24.25 RC Dermatologist V1, 06.12.25 RC Doctor, 05.29.25 RC Fired, 04.01.25 RC Christian Doctor, plus his Doctor Prompt in ryan-fire-prompts-2026-08-18.md.

## The hook shapes he actually uses (one per ad, never two)
1. The insider event: "I just got FIRED for telling a 67-year-old Woman there are 2 Types of 'Wrinkles'..." A thing happened today because the expert told a patient the truth. The secret is named by category, not explained.
2. Result first, contrarian: "Most of my Patients 'Fire' their Dermatologist after just 10 minutes in my office."
3. I can tell X by Y: "I can tell if a Woman has 'Wrinkles' just by looking at which Face-Wash she uses."
4. Thousands seen, only one works: "I've seen Thousands of 'Wrinkle' treatments in my 30 years... only ONE actually works Long-Term."
5. The industry crime: "Dermatologists only fix Half your 'Wrinkles'... and charge you for All of them."
6. The impossible result on the worst case: "I've reversed Wrinkles on 70-year-olds that Dermatologists said were 'Permanent'."
7. Do this right now: "Try Smiling for 5 seconds and you'll feel exactly how Wrinkles form."
8. What the expert gives his own family: "Ask any Derm: What would you Give your own Wife for Dark-Spots?"
9. The expert's own shame: "You're supposed to be a Dermatologist... Why can't you fix YOUR Dark-Spots?"
10. Third-party authority as the reveal: "what this Former Team Doctor for the Boston Celtics just figured out" / "Harvard Surgeon Names the #1 Prostate-Destroyer".

## The body, every time
- 100 to 180 words. One thought per line. White space.
- A NOT list of the things she already tried, three beats: "It's not Botox. That wears off in 3 months. It's not fillers. It's not the $300 creams at Sephora."
- The industry's motive in one line: "The cosmetic industry makes billions off repeat customers." / "Dermatologists are trained to push procedures."
- Credentials dropped mid-body, never in line one (the Fired ads open on the event, the 4X4 ads put "I've been an MD for over 30 years" two-thirds down).
- ONE mechanism nugget that explains why everything failed, said as a fact with a number, never taught: "two types of wrinkles, most creams only fix one", "a hidden melanin pathway", "something growing inside your prostate that your urologist isn't testing for".
- Curiosity by naming the category of the answer, not the answer: "It all has to do with how you wash your face. 98% of women get this wrong."
- The result in his clinic, with an age and a time: "women in their 60s and 70s with skin that looks 15 years younger", "70-year-olds".
- The deliverable is a thing he made: "I just put together my list of the Top 5... All of them work. But #1 on that list is the only one that...". For a video funnel: "watch this before you...".
- CTA is a command with a stake: "Click below to see my full Top 5 list now." / "Watch this right now before you waste another month." / "watch this before I delete it."
- Title Case on the key nouns, ONE quoted word, ONE caps word per hook, numbers everywhere, no product name, no price on the fix.

## What my first ProDenta doctor ads got wrong against this
- 200 words each and narrative; the corpus is 100 to 180 and punchy.
- No NOT list. No industry motive line. Mechanism half taught instead of named by category.
- Credentials in line one of the body.
- Reads like the squeeze page, not like a vet's Facebook post.

## 10-30 addendum (Olavita one-muscle doctor ads, Ryan: "way longer than our doctor ads... not how I would write... I wouldn't close with 'read it before you book the needle'")
- Measured: 4X4 Doctor AD 1-4 run 171 to 232 words WITH the hook; Doctor Stories AT1-3 120 to 161; Fired ads 87 to 114. His Doctor Prompt says 100 to 200. Write inside that.
- Read his Doctor Prompt in full first (ryan-fire-prompts-2026-08-18.md, "# Doctor Prompt": five examples, the never/always lists). The study above summarizes it; the prompt is the voice.
- The close is a command at the thing the doctor made or points to, with a parenthetical: "Click below to see my full Top 5 list now." / "Click below to read exactly what she did (before your next $600 appointment)." Never a soft advice line.
- A doctor ad is a Facebook post by a doctor, not a story told in a doctor's mouth. No week-by-week, no patient scenes stacked; one clinic flex, one patient, one mechanism nugget.
