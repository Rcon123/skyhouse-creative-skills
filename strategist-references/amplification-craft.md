# HOOK AMPLIFICATION — THE CRAFT PASS
(Source: Luke Iha's "Exodus Masterclass V.1" kit, shared in #the-creative-cartel 2026-08-25; distilled from the Genesis Live Training hook-amplification system, 2026-08-17. Adopted into the SkyHouse hook stack 2026-08-25, PROVISIONAL until Ryan grades the integration. Runs AFTER hook-laws.md; RULE 0 in hook-laws outranks everything here — clarity gates run last and kill anything the voltage pass bent.)

Your job in this pass: take the hook pools you are given, mine them for the strongest IDEAS, and AMPLIFY those ideas. Bangers are ideas, not sentences: intensity multiplies a good idea and never rescues a bad one. Most of the real work is killing your own versions for named reasons.

## THE PHYSICS
- A hook has one job: make the right person feel an irresistible need to consume the very next sentence. It works if they think "Wait... WHAT?" / "That's me." / "I need to know." It fails if they think "interesting" — interest scrolls.
- Hooks detonate, they don't create: they trigger emotion the reader already carries (years of faithful use, money spent, self-blame in the mirror, advice they obeyed that never worked). A hook aimed at nothing stored is loud and dead.
- Anger converts. Shame paralyzes. Every strong hook hands the reader absolution: your past failures were never your fault, and now there's a named reason why.
- Recognition is not compulsion: a line that makes someone feel perfectly SEEN, but complete, is a mirror — he nods and keeps scrolling. Leave the loop OPEN.
- GOOD hooks are hyper-specific validation of a valence flip: something perceived bad revealed as secretly good, or something perceived good revealed as secretly and ACTIVELY harmful — never merely "ineffective"; ineffective makes nobody flinch. GREAT hooks do all that AND open a loop the prospect feels chemically compelled to close. The test at the end of every hook: what specific question is the reader left holding? None = you wrote a diagnosis, not a hook.
- The core equation: Attention = Relevance x (Curiosity + Tension + Value + Novelty) x Credibility. Amplifiers can never substitute for the core. Grade the core trio FIRST.
- THE TRUTH ANCHOR (caps every dial): amplify FRAMING, never facts. The most charged TRUE framing is legal ("flavored SEAWATER", "fake gold"); an invented claim never is. Any number or absolute that ships needs a source a hostile reader can't break — flag it.

## THE PROTOCOL (run on each hook)
0. AIM IT: which suspicion does this market already carry? Whose enemy gets the rock? Which past failure gets justified? Which identity is under threat — not the symptom, the kneeling in the garden, the kid's respect, the marriage.
1. FREEZE THE IDEA as one ugly sentence. If the market wouldn't stop for the boring version, find a better idea — do not polish a dud. A stronger neighboring idea is a DIFFERENT hook — park it and climb it separately.
2. THE INEFFECTIVE GATE: an idea shaped like "X doesn't work" may not enter. Escalate first: what's IN it, what it KEPT them on, what it let PROGRESS, or what prize it steals outright.
3. CLIMB — amplify the FACT, not the sentence: each version must assert a STRONGER CLAIM than the last. Same claim in prettier clothes = expression dressing, still weak. Stakes ladder: wallet, body, brain/irreversible, identity/relationship.
4. HUNT THE CONNOTATION: what is this crime ALREADY CALLED in everyday life? Name the criminal's trade, not just the crime.
5. RENDER AS SPEECH: the bar test — if you couldn't say the line at a bar exactly as written, it isn't rendered. Caveman words. Protect the charged word. Collision by word 10-12; stop at the hardest word.
6. THE LOOP GATE: write down the specific question the reader is left holding. None = put the mystery back.
7. THE CORE-FIRST AUDIT: grade relevance, tension, value BEFORE admiring craft. Does it make YOU flinch?

## THE FOUR MOVES (every killer hook contains all four)
1. THE WORST TRUTH: the worst thing that's TRUE here, never "doesn't work."
2. THEIR MOMENT: what moment of theirs does this line make them SEE? Nobody escapes with "that's not me."
3. THE FACE: what is this crime already CALLED, and who's doing it? Best: catch the villain performing after the crime.
4. THE ITCH: what must they find out, and can you say the line at a bar?

## THE DIALS (a finished hook carries 4-6, never more)
Character: charge-stacking, the unlikely messenger, the witness. Scene: named mundane setting, the relic object, exact clocks. Villain: enemy inversion (the helper is the cause), authority-failure clause, crime verb, coined enemy. Stakes: consequence magnitude, suffering duration, irreversible loss, load-bearing target (the marriage, not the symptom). Language: charged-word protection, caveman words, velocity. Loop: withhold the cause, paradox in one breath, self-verification, edge of reality. Also proven: present tense as a dial, the receipt, composure ("she didn't yell" is scarier than yelling), the category hijack, the irony knife, the calibrated hedge on the biggest claim.

## CEILING — CRANK VS FORK
Not every hook has headroom; at ceiling, more voltage makes it worse. Fork instead: the family sibling (same idea, different awareness door), the symptom door (enter through a lived moment, then redirect the blame), or the POV re-skin (self / spouse / mother). Craft and spend do not prove stakes — run the who-gives-a-fuck check; rescue trivial crimes by prize theft, never louder outrage.

## OUTPUT CONTRACT
For each delivered hook: the frozen ugly sentence, the settled amplified line, the dials in use, the specific question the reader is left holding, its Level grade. Rotate vehicles — no two adjacent hooks share a skeleton. Flag any number or absolute needing a source.
