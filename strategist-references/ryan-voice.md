## THE FORMAT TAXONOMY (get this right first; wrong format = dead on arrival)

**1. COLD AD (feeds an advertorial/VSL/listicle). 60-130 words. His workhorse.**
Structure: SHOCK OPENER (first-person or character claim: "I just got FIRED for telling a 67-year-old Woman there are 2 Types of 'Wrinkles'...") -> QUALIFIER ADDRESS ("Ladies..." / "Men... if you're up 5 times a night and nothing your doctor gave you has worked...") -> URGENT COMMAND ("You literally need to watch this immediately.") -> AUTHORITY + EMOTION ("He worked with the Boston Celtics for years and he's FURIOUS about what's happening to men.") -> MECHANISM TEASE, never the lesson ("There's something growing inside your prostate that your urologist isn't testing for.") -> STAKES LIST ("The sleepless nights. The destroyed sex life. All of it.") -> COMMAND CTA ("Do NOT take another pill until you watch this…" / "PLEASE watch this before you waste another dollar…" / "watch this before I delete it.")
THE AD SELLS THE CLICK. It never teaches the full mechanism, never names the product, never closes the sale. Curiosity stays open.

**2. RETARGETING AT (warm, product named). 130-200 words.**
Direct claim opener ("Testosterone boosters don't work. Here's what actually does.") -> plain mechanism ("It needs the raw materials to make testosterone. Period.") -> product named casually -> blunt benefit list ("Morning wood. Strength in the gym. Sex drive that exists.") -> social proof number -> "Tap below."

GRADED CORRECTIONS (2026-07-23, morning wood session, round 2): (1) REFERENT RULE: never write a line whose meaning depends on an earlier line. Restate the thing by name (morning wood, the chest, the pills) every single time. "The only thing I risked was admitting my wife was right" failed because right about WHAT is not in the sentence. Ryan: "never assume they understand what you're talking about, always remind them." (2) SYMPTOM PSYCHOLOGY RULE: the buyer ALWAYS noticed his own symptom. "He didn't notice it was gone" is false psychology and graded out. The true emotion is SHAME: he noticed long ago, lied to himself (tired, stressed, older), told nobody, and the gut punch is someone else saying it out loud. Failed solutions happen in secret (Googled with the door shut). (3) Failed solutions must be what buyers actually try, in their words (little blue pills, vitamin-aisle T-booster, earlier nights, less beer). Zinc pills graded out as not a thing buyers know.

## THE SENTENCE RULES (from his own SOP, verbatim)

His Natural Language Prompt: "Do NOT use staccato sentences like this: 'That's not metaphor. That's basic physiology.' Instead, be more natural: 'That's not even a metaphor. That's actually just how the body works.' Focus on the natural, conversational flow and musicality. You MUST use full grammatical sentences always."
His AI-tell ban list (SOP, abridged): predictable statement-then-colon structures; balanced "This isn't X -- it's Y" constructions; three-part lists; formal transitions (However/Moreover/Furthermore); "Not just X, but Y"; "The key is:"/"The truth is:"; "Picture this:"; business jargon (leverage/optimize/transform/game-changing); journey metaphors (delve/dive/embark/navigate).
His human-pattern list (SOP): varied paragraph lengths; fragments and starting with "And/But/So"; conversational asides and parentheticals; minor imperfections; clear opinions without hedging; sudden spontaneous questions; vary sentence length significantly.
His simplify rule (SOP, verbatim spirit): swap "high falutin latin" words for "concrete, earthy" Germanic words; "brute caveman simple," 3rd-5th grade, without losing intensity.
His edit verbs: CUT don't compress; be ANAL about fluff that doesn't move the argument; WEAVE proof and concrete visual language; INTENSIFY pain and benefit language.
NOTE: he does use negation stacks ("It's not stress. It's not age.") and short beats, but as seasoning with intent, never as the default sentence shape. The default shape is a natural spoken sentence.

## HIS DEVICES (observed across hundreds of docs)

- Headline devices: (This) / (See How) / (Their Labs Prove it…) parentheticals; ONE quoted provocative word ("Botox", "Wrinkle", "Prostate-Flush"); ONE caps word (FURIOUS, ERASE, NEVER); numbers everywhere (67, 2X, 170,000+, $8,000, 48 hours, top 5, #1, 2026); retailer/authority anchors (Target, Macy's, Harvard Surgeon, NBA Doctor, Boston Celtics).
- Testimonial-as-headline in quotes: "More Sex. Better Sex. My Wife is the Happiest She's Been in Years."
- Visceral symptom specifics, never generic: "standing at the toilet for 2 minutes", "that warm trickle down your leg after you zip up", "up at midnight, 2am, 4am, and 5:30".
- Social-reaction proof: "My husband can't stop staring at me." "People literally stop her in public." "Their wives are asking what changed."
- Emotional authenticity beats: "(I'm not making this up...)" / "(She's mortified lol)" / "And I'm pissed I didn't know about this sooner." / "she started crying. Hugged me. Then I got fired."
- Righteous anger at the system: doctors/urologists/industry "have NO IDEA", are "a DECADE behind", "FURIOUS they didn't know sooner". Blame removal built in: "It's not your fault... but you need to see this immediately."
- The discovery device: "My friend sent me a ranking of skincare brands that actually work... and the top one changed my life." (friend/article/ranking, then "skip to number one")
- Command CTAs with urgency + empathy: "Stop what you're doing and watch this right now." / "watch this before you accept that."
- Casual profanity where it lands: "Stop wasting money on expensive shit that doesn't work and click it." / "So fuck it. I'm telling everyone." / "still in the toilet".
- Dry wit: "boosters that boost nothing", "Sex drive that exists."

GRADED CORRECTION (2026-08-04, TPS whiteboard video session): (1) VAGUE-NOUN RULE, an extension of the referent rule: every noun must be fully named IN the sentence, even scene-setting ones. "Nobody at the counter will ever explain this" failed because counter of WHAT is not in the sentence. Ryan: "What the fuck does that mean? What counter?" If a stranger reading one sentence alone can't picture the thing, rewrite it. (2) HOOKS CARRY THE BENEFIT: a mechanism fact alone ("there are two kinds of wrinkles") is not a hook; the reader must be told what's in it for them inside the hook itself (the deep lines around your mouth can fade, in about three weeks, for less than dinner). Mechanism is the curiosity half, never the whole hook. (3) FIRE HOOKS ARE SCENES, NOT CLAIMS: every hook Ryan calls fire is a dramatic moment happening to a person (an accusation, a confrontation, a reaction: "Dermatologist is PISSED", "Ex-Husband's new Girlfriend begs"). A claim-shaped hook ("half your wrinkles have never been treated") is structurally not fire no matter how short or true. When the format bans first person, tell the scene in third person.

## THE KILL LIST (never do)

- Analogies/metaphors (no paychecks, no factories-as-metaphor, no costumes). He states mechanisms literally.
- Poetic abstractions ("the wanting", "the pull"). Say sex drive, want sex, morning wood.
- Constant two-beat staccato (his own SOP's #1 ban). Full grammatical flowing sentences by default.
- Teaching the whole mechanism in a COLD ad. Tease it; the lander teaches.
- Naming the product in a COLD ad. "The #1 on his list" / "what this doctor figured out."
- Ads over ~130 words for cold, ~200 for retargeting.
- Em dashes. More than one CAPS word or quoted word per hook. Hedged verbs.
