# VSL gem mining: how to pull ad and lander angles out of a video sales letter

PROVISIONAL. Written 2026-09-14 from the TestoGreens food work. Ryan has not graded this wording yet.

## What a gem is

A gem is one line in the VSL that you can pin to a thing the buyer already owns and already has an opinion about.

- The ad shows the thing.
- The lander answers the question the ad raised, using the VSL's own words.
- The video keeps the big reveal.

## Why this works (the proof it is built on)

TestoGreens, September 2026:

1. The VSL opens with "beware of these 5 popular foods" and never names them. The Top 5 Foods lander fills that hole. Same claim: 1.75 ROAS through the lander, 0.59 straight to the video.
2. Coke vs Pepsi was the best image the account has run: $33 per checkout. Four things made it work:
   - He already has a side.
   - The two are the same kind of thing, so he cannot guess the answer.
   - It reads at thumbnail size with no words.
   - He WANTS the answer to be the other one.
3. Bud Light vs Coors Light brought the best buyer (7.5% of lander clicks started a checkout, versus 3.1% for Coke vs Pepsi). But the page lost a third more of those men, because beer was not on the page.
4. Fries vs burger got the best click rate (12.5%) and the worst cost per sale ($395). A click is not a buyer. Always grade on checkouts.

## Step 0. The source is the VSL itself

Mine only the VSL script unless Ryan asks for more. Do NOT mix in the client's own Facebook ads or competitor ads: they are not ours and not the video (Ryan, 2026-09-14, after v2 of the TestoGreens gems pulled hops, canola and almonds from Live Anabolic's in-house ads). If another source is ever used, label it by name on every gem.

Start with the VSL's opening twist. The lead usually says what kind of thing the villain is (on TestoGreens: "foods you think are fueling testosterone" [19 to 33]). Check that the live landers match that twist before hunting anything new.

## The three moves that find the best gems

1. **The opening twist, made physical.** Find the one object that fits the lead's twist exactly, using a line from later in the VSL as the reason. (Foods men eat FOR testosterone backfire [23], plus "farm animals pumped with estrogen" [1011]: the steak.)
2. **The doctor's betrayal.** Pair what the VSL says he was told is healthy with what it says he actually needs. (Oatmeal vs bacon and eggs, from "diets designed for women" [991 to 999].)
3. **Best image, dressed in a VSL line.** Take the offer's best-ever image shape and find a VSL line that fits it. (The polaroid won, and the VSL says "since your honeymoon": a honeymoon polaroid.)

## Step 1. Read the whole VSL and pull six kinds of lines

Read every line, including the FAQ after the close. The FAQ is where the best objects hide.

| Kind | What to look for | TestoGreens example |
|---|---|---|
| **Unpaid promise** | The VSL says a list exists and never gives it. Each one is a Top 5 lander. | "5 popular foods" (never named). "Thousands of chemicals in everyday household items" (only 3 named). |
| **Named object** | Every noun he can hold in his hand. | Plastic water bottle, non-stick pan, coffee cups, tap water, beer belly. |
| **A picture the doctor already drew** | A comparison or image in the script that is already a photo. | "A pinch of salt on a steak" vs "the whole shaker." "Testicles the size of raisins." |
| **A number he can find himself in** | Age ladders, ranges, "if you're 60..." | 40 = 35% more, 50 = 70% more, 60 = double. Grandfather's 1,000 to 1,500 vs today's 250 to 650. |
| **Betrayal or permission** | Things he was told were healthy that the VSL says hurt him, and things he was told to cut that the VSL says he needs. | "Diets designed for women." "Swap steak, eggs, butter and bacon for greens and grains and your body has nothing to make testosterone from." |
| **A name with charge** | A nickname or phrase that fights a culture fight he is already in. | "Swedish scientists call it the Gender Confusion Enzyme." |

Write down the line number for every pull. No line number, no gem.

## Step 2. Pin it to an object he really touches

Check the offer's audience facts before keeping any object (for TestoGreens: `offers/testogreens/research/MEN-65-PLUS-WHAT-THEY-EAT-2026-09-10.md`). If a 65-year-old man does not touch it every week, it is out, no matter how good the line is.

## Step 3. Score it on five tests (0 or 1 each)

1. **Side.** He already picked one of the two.
2. **Cannot guess.** The two are close enough that the answer is not obvious. (Coke vs Diet Coke failed this and cost three times as much per checkout.)
3. **Thumbnail.** It reads with no words.
4. **Wants it guilty.** He hopes a specific answer is true. This is the strongest of the five.
5. **Page pays.** The lander can answer it in three lines using a VSL line, without spending the video's reveal.

Single objects with a note on them died in the TestoGreens test ($447, one sale). If a gem cannot become a pair or a list, it is copy, not an image.

## Step 4. Kill checks

- Product-aware (bottle, brand, price, ingredient pitch) on a VSL offer: out.
- The claim is not in the VSL: mark it BET, never present it as VSL-backed.
- On the offer's dead list (TestoGreens: morning wood, man boobs, milk, "just cut foods, no pills"): out.
- Names a real brand as harmful beyond what the VSL says: flag for Ryan.

## Step 5. Write each gem the same way

- **VSL line** (with line number)
- **The object**
- **The ad:** the image in one sentence, plus the first line of ad text
- **The lander:** a slot on an existing page, or a whole new page
- **Score** (out of 5) and **what already backs it** (account numbers or market numbers)
- **Risk** in one line

## The one rule that ties it together

Every object in an ad image must appear on the page that ad lands on. The beer campaign proved it.
