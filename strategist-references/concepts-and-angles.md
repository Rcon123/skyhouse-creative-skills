## The system in one line

Collect discrete FACTS about the audience, the market, and the product. Facts are not messages. Wrap a fact in an angle (breaking news, confession, discovery, conspiracy) and you get a message that stops the scroll. Fact pantry in, ads out.

## The three fact mines + toolkit

### 1. AVATAR FACTS (raw truths about the audience)

**A. Identity**: demographics; self-concept deep beliefs ("I am someone who used to be athletic but now my body is betraying me"); core problem statement; cultural touchstones; meaningful symbols (the empty Advil bottle, the hidden bathroom scale).

**B. Experience (how they live the problem)**:
- **Pain Expressions ladder**: General > Specific > Cinematic > Emotional > Core Wound. ("I'm tired" > "can't make it past 2pm" > "sitting in the parking lot crying before work" > "I'm disappearing" > "I'll die having never really lived.") Write at the cinematic level or deeper.
- **Pain Matrix** (rate 1-10): source, causal clarity, visibility ("but you look fine"), urgency, stigma, measurability, frequency, locus of control, emotional intensity, self vs others.
- Pain evolution (early warning > building > crisis > emotional nadir); core wound analysis (what ontological resource they fear losing: vitality, independence, professional identity, parental role, desirability); how the pain ripples to others; what they see / hear / say / think / feel / do; behavioral patterns; **buying triggers** (the moments that make them ready to buy); language patterns; things they refuse to do; casual cruelties others say.

**C. Motivation**: Promise ladder (General > Specific > Cinematic > Emotional > Core: "feel better" > "wake up refreshed" > "jump out of bed excited" > "feel alive again" > "become who I was meant to be"); desire depth stack (surface want > why > deeper > core need); transformation vision (before/after identity); status needs; security needs; relationship impact; social consequences.

**D. Resistance (what stops them)**: identity constraints ("I'm not someone who complains"); values constraints ("natural is better"); belief constraints ("nothing works for me"); experience constraints ("last one made it worse"); resource constraints; **core objections**; **secondary gains** (the hidden benefit of keeping the problem); who/what they blame.

**E. Media consumption**: platforms, content types, influencers, trusted publications.

**F. Intensification cascades**: physical, financial, relationship, identity spirals (each step worse); and the mirror transformation cascades (each step better). Great for stakes and after-states.

**G. Other patterns**: crisis moments (3am, milestone birthdays, tax season); comparison triggers; seasonal patterns; hope-despair loop; shame spiral; motivation cycle (morning optimism > 2am research); scroll-stopping patterns: weird facts ("why you wake at 3:17am"), magic words ("the 4-word text"), weird methods ("the Japanese loophole"), loopholes and hacks.

### 2. MARKET FACTS (the landscape)

**H. Solutions**: leaders (who dominates and at what price), alternatives and DIY workarounds, emerging entrants, what the market values, what it instantly rejects (deal-breakers), **trust signals** (what proof actually moves them vs gets dismissed), promises competitors make, how competitors position the problem, their USPs, price points and offers.

**I. Failures**: what everyone tries first, why it fails (lived breaking points, not theory), what they like/dislike about failed solutions, **cost of failure** (sunk time and money = skepticism), wrong advice from "experts", harmful remedies that deepen the problem.

**J. Influences**: real authorities they trust (and fake ones they dismiss), trending narratives, cultural products (documentaries, podcasts, movements), generational shifts, economic climate, reactive counter-movements, viral phenomena, news and studies, proof stories being passed around, forums and groups they sit in, **insider language** (the tribe's acronyms), **sacred cows** (never challenge these inside the tribe), tribal enemies and allies.

**K. Evaluation**: where and how they research, decision criteria in order of actual importance.

### 3. PRODUCT FACTS (the solution)

**L. Solution story**: trigger events (the enough-is-enough moment), UMP discovery (the problem behind the problem), UMS discovery (the unique solution moment), attempts and failures on the way, breakthrough moment, first success story, unsolicited requests, refinement, current form.

**M. Mechanism**: **UMP** (hidden mechanism explaining why the surface problem exists and why conventional fixes fail), root cause (the surprising culprit), **UMS** (the specific mechanism by which your solution works), key components, scientific/historical backing, unique advantage, simplicity factor.

**N. Diagnostics**: problem quizzes, hidden-mechanism checks, constraint identifiers, readiness checks, archetype matches. (Quizzes create progressive buy-in.)

**O. Transformation path**: stage progression, milestone markers, realistic time expectations.

**P. Implementation**: protocols, habit installation, environment design, tools, accountability, progress tracking, common failure points, recovery protocols, plateau breakers.

**Q. Offer details**: modules, format/delivery, duration/access, features + benefits, bonuses, support, tools included, guarantees, pricing, payment options, legitimate urgency.

**R. Competitive differentiation**: vs leaders, vs alternatives, vs DIY/free, what only we offer, speed/ease advantages, result differentials.

**S. Social proof**: testimonials, case studies, before/after, video proof, screenshots, metrics, transformations.

**T. Philosophy & culture**: core truths, paradigm shifts (from "I'm bad with money" to "I have money trauma"), what we stand for/against, rituals, symbols, community standards, mission.

## PSYCHOLOGY TOOLKIT (reshaping beliefs)

Every sale requires belief transformation across three models: **PROBLEM MODEL** (what's really wrong), **SOLUTION MODEL** (what actually works), **VENDOR MODEL** (why you specifically). Four operations: **UNINSTALL** limiting beliefs, **INSTALL** empowering ones, **ACCOMMODATE** existing ones ("yes diet matters, but..."), **REPLACE** one with another.

### The 7 Critical Beliefs (primary targets)

1. **Unique Mechanism**: from "my problem is caused by [surface symptom]" to "caused by [specific mechanism] only this addresses"
2. **Failed Alternatives**: from "I've tried everything / need more willpower" to "what I tried was doomed because it didn't address [mechanism]"
3. **Escalating Cost**: from "annoying but manageable" to "actively getting worse, costing me daily"
4. **Constraint Removal**: from "I don't have the time/money/genetics" to "I already have everything needed"
5. **Personal Authority**: from "another guru" to "discovered it through struggle, helped thousands"
6. **Risk Reversal**: from "I'll waste my money like always" to "works or costs nothing"
7. **Now-or-Never**: from "maybe later" to "this opportunity won't remain available"

### Transformation techniques (apply a technique to a target belief, get a usable concept)

- **Metaphors & analogies**: "your metabolism is a furnace with a broken thermostat, eating less just makes it burn colder"
- **Paradoxical questions**: "why do farmers in Nepal carry 100lbs daily with no back pain while office workers can't bend over?"
- **Characterizations (naming)**: "Tired But Wired Syndrome", "the Hope Marketing Hamster Wheel", "Achievement Quicksand"
- **Testable proof**: "take your morning temperature for 3 days, under 97.8? Your thyroid is struggling despite normal tests"
- **Causation reframes**: reverse causation ("you're 'lazy' because you're metabolically broken, the fatigue came first"); hidden causes (C causes both A and B); circular causation (A and B feed each other, break any link); threshold causation (A only causes B past point X); vicious cycles; cross-system feedback; hidden constraints ("the real limiting factor is X"); leverage points ("a 3-second adjustment matters 100x more than $10,000 of equipment")
- **Constraint-dissolution formulas**: scientific evidence, historical examples (Mailchimp/Spanx for "I need VC"), paradox, thought experiment, personal challenge, consequences ("if some people truly couldn't lose weight they'd violate thermodynamics"), analogy, current events, storytelling, counterintuitive wisdom

### Ready-to-use concept shapes

Surprising culprits ("your 'healthy' smoothie spikes insulin harder than a donut"); quizzes and diagnostics; trust violations / personal exposure ("my husband doesn't know I spent $2,000 on this"); hierarchy violations ("I told the head of Johns Hopkins fertility he was wrong"); privacy violations / biological transparency ("my actual labs: 180 to 890 in 90 days"); threat detection; resource scarcity; sexual/status competition; incomplete loops; pattern violations ("the gym teacher who can't do a pushup"); mystery mechanisms ("the purple food that reverses aging"); conflict documentation ("doctor fired for revealing this"); tribal violations ("I was kicked out of the keto community"); status disruption ("why I returned my Tesla for a 1992 Corolla"); trending and current events.

# PART 2: ANGLES (added 2026-08-03, the half this file was missing)

**This file previously contained only the fact-mining half of the source doc. The angles taxonomy below was absent, and its absence caused a real analysis failure on the Akka liver market: outcomes and concepts were reported as "angles," which made it impossible to tell whether the market was voting for the result, the fact, or the framing.** Full source PDF: `strategist-references/source/concepts-and-angles-full.pdf`.

## The distinction, and why it decides everything

- **OUTCOME** = the result promised (weight, cholesterol, energy). Not an angle.
- **CONCEPT** = the raw fact asserted ("cortisol is what's keeping you fat", "cholesterol is a liver problem"). **Finite. Expensive to prove. The market votes on these with money.**
- **ANGLE** = the psychological frame wrapped around the fact (whistleblower, conspiracy, geographic, accidental discovery, countdown). **Infinite. Cheap.**

> Concepts are finite. Angles are infinite. Spend your firepower on angles.

Angles matter because we don't change minds with new information, we change HOW people attend to information they already have. The angle is the psychological premise that makes someone CARE about information they would otherwise ignore.

**The multiplication effect.** One concept x many angles = many completely different psychological experiences. Base concept "vitamin D deficiency causes depression" becomes: Conspiracy ("why psychiatrists never check your vitamin D, it would end their practice") / Geographic ("why nobody in Dubai gets seasonal depression") / Accidental Discovery ("how a lab error revealed vitamin D's effect on serotonin") / Countdown ("FDA banning high-dose vitamin D in 30 days") / Enemy Admission ("even antidepressant manufacturers tell employees to take vitamin D"). Same information, five reasons to care right now.

## THE REMIX RULE (the highest-leverage move in the pack)

Read the pattern first. **One winner sharing a trait is an anecdote. Three is a pattern.** Then:

- **If a CONCEPT is winning, hold the concept and rotate the ANGLES.** The market has told you it cares about the fact. Try every door into it.
- **If an ANGLE is winning, hold the angle and rotate the CONCEPTS.** The market has told you which key opens the lock. Try that key on every door.

Confidence scales with the evidence underwriting the pattern. Hooks are refreshed on top of everything else always, because hooks are the cheapest variation there is. **Rewording a headline is NOT a remix.** If the concept and the angle both stay fixed and only the wording moves, that is hook refreshing, and an account doing only that has an untouched angle layer.

## How the strategist tool must use this

- **Never call an outcome or a concept an "angle."** When reporting what a market is testing, break out all three layers separately and say which layer the wins are actually sitting in. Collapsing them makes the analysis useless for deciding what to build.
- When dissecting an account, build the **concept x angle grid**: proven concepts down the side, the 55 angles across. Occupied cells are what has been paid for. Empty cells on a proven-concept row are the cheapest ads available anywhere.
- Batch specs name the concept AND the angle as separate fields.
- Angle count per brand is a health metric. A brand with hundreds of variants inside one or two angles has a monoculture, and monocultures fatigue.
