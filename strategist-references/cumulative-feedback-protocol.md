# CUMULATIVE FEEDBACK + NO REGRESSION PROTOCOL
(Ryan's verbatim directive, 2026-08-17. Standing law for ALL creative work: hooks, briefs, copy, images. Required reading alongside hook-laws.md before any creative session.)

When Ryan gives creative feedback, NEVER treat his newest comment as the only instruction. His feedback is CUMULATIVE. Every correction adds a new rule to the existing rules unless Ryan explicitly tells you to replace or remove an old one. Your job is NOT to fix the latest complaint while accidentally reintroducing problems he already corrected. Before showing Ryan any new copy, silently check it against ALL feedback given so far in the conversation.

THE #1 RULE: DO NOT FIX ONE THING BY BREAKING ANOTHER THING. If Ryan says: make it less vague, make the benefit clearer, explain why this person would know this, make it 4th-grade reading level, don't reveal the mechanism, keep this opening, make it more dramatic... the next version must do ALL SEVEN. Not just the most recent one.

LOCK WHAT RYAN APPROVES. When Ryan says "this is good," "I like #5," "the beginning is good," "keep this," "getting much closer," "this part works": the approved element is LOCKED. Do not casually rewrite it in the next version. Only change the specific part Ryan criticized. Preserve approved wording verbatim unless he specifically asks to change it.

CURIOSITY DOES NOT MEAN VAGUE. Do NOT create curiosity with empty words (something, it, this, they, what happened, what changed, what we lost, the answer, the difference, a secret, one thing, a discovery) when the viewer has no clear idea what CATEGORY of thing is meant. Ryan wants: maximum concrete information around ONE withheld reveal. The viewer should understand the entire situation while still having one very specific unanswered question. BAD: "They did something to you as a baby that changed your body forever" (five gaps, confusion). BETTER: "Starting in the 1950s, doctors began removing something from babies that helped their bodies stay naturally lean." (WHO: doctors. WHEN: 1950s. EVENT TYPE: removal. WHO IT HAPPENED TO: babies. WHY I CARE: staying lean. ONE remaining question: what did they remove?)

BENEFIT / WHY SHOULD I CARE comes EARLY. On a belly-fat ad, weight/belly/staying lean appears extremely early, within the first sentence or two. Never save relevance for the last line.

ALWAYS ANSWER: WHY IS THIS RANDOM PERSON TELLING ME THIS? The avatar's knowledge must be believable: profession, personal experience, research they did, records/photos found, a credible person who showed them, firsthand result. But do NOT jam boring credentials into the hook just to check a box ("a gut doctor showed me" can weaken a hook). Credibility must feel natural to the character and story. AND distinguish: (1) why she KNOWS vs (2) why she's TELLING you. "I'm telling every woman I know" is motive, not knowledge.

WRITE LIKE A NORMAL PERSON TALKS. 4th-grade level does NOT mean robotic, choppy, incomplete, compressed, childish, or vague. Avoid unnatural lines ("Their bodies broke fat down with a fluid ours barely make", "Their bodies burned it off. Ours don't."). Use simple WORDS and simple STRUCTURE, but never mutilate normal speech to hit a reading level. Test: would a real person naturally say this exact sentence while telling a friend something shocking?

SPECIFICITY > CLEVERNESS. Crystal-clear beats clever-sounding. No compressed phrases needing interpretation. No half-second of "wait... what does that mean?", especially in hooks.

DON'T INVENT FACTS TO SOLVE COPY PROBLEMS. Never turn a research inference into stated fact. If the VSL says "a single, common medical event" and does not say medicine/antibiotics/procedure, the copy may NOT say "a common medicine." Separate: what the source explicitly says / what research suggests / what we infer. Never smuggle an inference into copy as fact.

RESPECT THE FUNNEL STAGE. Know the actual click path (AD then ADVERTORIAL then VSL). The ad creates curiosity for THE NEXT PAGE only. Determine what the ad can reveal, what the advertorial reveals, what stays hidden until the VSL. Never skip ahead.

HOOK CHECKLIST (verify ALL silently before showing any hook):
1. Grabs in the FIRST FEW WORDS?
2. Problem/benefit obvious very early?
3. Concrete, not vague?
4. ONE compelling curiosity gap, not five confusing ones?
5. Who/what/when clear enough to follow instantly?
6. Believable reason the avatar knows this?
7. Avatar sounds like a real person?
8. 4th-grader easy?
9. No awkward or compressed phrasing?
10. Every previously approved element preserved?
11. Obeys ALL earlier feedback, not just the latest message?
12. Reveals nothing the next funnel step is supposed to reveal?
13. Every factual claim supported by source material?
14. Viewer immediately knows why they should care?
If a hook fails even ONE, fix it BEFORE showing Ryan.

WHEN RYAN GIVES FEEDBACK ON A BATCH: do not immediately regenerate. First silently translate his feedback into persistent rules, then apply them to every following batch.

MOST IMPORTANT MENTAL MODEL: Ryan is progressively dialing in the SAME creative target, not asking for reinvention each round. Version 2 = everything that worked in Version 1 + his newest correction, minus the thing he disliked. The goal is convergence. Never regress into an issue already corrected.

# V2: RYAN CREATIVE REVISION MODE (Ryan's verbatim update, 2026-08-17. This version governs; v1 above remains as background.)

The biggest rule: When Ryan gives feedback, change ONLY what his feedback requires. Preserve everything else that was already working. Do not regenerate from scratch every round. Think: Previous best version + new correction = next version. NOT: New feedback = new interpretation of the whole assignment.

Maintain 4 things silently before writing:
1. HARD RULES (apply across the creative): clear natural spoken English; about 4th-grade reading level but never dumb/choppy; strong first few words; viewer quickly understands why they should care; specificity creates curiosity, vagueness does not; never invent facts.
2. LOCKED ELEMENTS: anything Ryan explicitly approved ("this is good," "I like the first part," "keep this," "#2 is the only good one") is locked wording/idea. Do not change a locked element unless Ryan specifically asks.
3. FACTS + REVEAL BOUNDARY: know what the source actually says, what the next page says, what can be teased, what cannot be revealed yet. Never turn an inference into a fact because it makes better copy.
4. CURRENT CHANGE: what exactly is Ryan asking to improve RIGHT NOW? Change that dimension while preserving 1-3.

IMPORTANT, DO NOT OVER-APPLY FEEDBACK: feedback is cumulative but that does NOT mean every rule is literally stated inside every hook. Understand the PURPOSE. "How does she know this?" = the audience needs a believable reason she has the information; it does NOT mean "I'm a retired nurse" in every opening; if the credential weakens the hook, establish it naturally immediately afterward. "What's in it for me?" = relevance to belly fat/weight becomes clear FAST; not the literal words "belly fat" in the first five words. Apply the principle intelligently.

CURIOSITY: do not confuse curiosity with missing information. BAD: "They did something to you." / "Something changed." / "I found out what happened." GOOD curiosity gives a CONCRETE premise and withholds the most interesting answer. Viewer thinks "Wait... WHAT did they remove?" not "what is she even talking about?" Vague words (it, this, something, they, the answer, what changed) are only allowed when the sentence already gave them a clear concrete meaning.

WHEN RYAN ASKS FOR "HARDER": do not become cleverer, more cryptic, more dramatic-sounding, choppier, or sensational-but-unclear. Harder means: get to the most surprising, specific, relevant fact faster.

WHEN RYAN ASKS FOR "SHORTER": cut filler, throat-clearing, repetition, unnecessary explanation, weak transitions. Do NOT cut the concrete premise, the reason the viewer cares, necessary context, or the logic that makes the sentence make sense. Shorter means denser, not vaguer.

NORMAL SPEECH RULE: never sacrifice normal human speech for brevity. If it sounds like copywriting shorthand instead of something a real person would say out loud, rewrite. 4th-grade = common words, easy structure, instant comprehension. NOT fragments, caveman phrasing, unnatural compression.

REVISION RULE: when Ryan criticizes ONE PART of a line, preserve the approved part verbatim and rewrite only the bad part. Do not give three new versions of the approved half. Do not reinterpret the concept. Do not add new angles. Solve the problem he pointed at.

FINAL SILENT CHECK before showing copy: 1) Did I preserve what Ryan already approved? 2) Did I actually fix the thing he just complained about? 3) Is anything vague, weird, or requiring interpretation? 4) Did I accidentally break an older rule while fixing this one? If yes, fix before responding. The goal is CONVERGENCE: every round narrows toward the target instead of restarting.

# V2 ADDENDUM (Ryan verbatim, 2026-08-17): CONTINUATION RULE + FAKE SPECIFICITY + REFERENT TEST + ONE-THOUGHT TEST

CONTINUATION RULE: when Ryan approves the first half of a hook and asks for a fixed second half, do NOT treat the second half as a place to add another supporting fact. The second half must CONTINUE and intensify the exact thought already established. Ask: what does the viewer naturally need to hear NEXT after this sentence? Not: what other fact can I add? Do not introduce a new historical claim, generation comparison, character, proof point, mechanism, testimonial claim, or causal claim unless Ryan asks. The second half usually performs ONE job: make the existing claim personally relevant; make the existing consequence clearer; naturally establish why the speaker knows; sharpen the payoff; deepen the SAME curiosity gap. It should not open another idea.

NEVER CREATE FAKE SPECIFICITY: specific does not mean a broader or stronger claim. "Babies before 1955 stayed thin. Babies after didn't." / "Our grandparents kept it their whole lives." / "Women getting it back are losing belly fat." are NEW factual claims, not clearer wording. Only use specificity actually supported by the source. When facts run out, improve through clearer framing, relevance, consequence, or natural speech. Never fill an information gap by inventing details.

REFERENT TEST: never use it / theirs / this / that / what we lost / getting it back unless a normal viewer instantly knows exactly what the word refers to. If Ryan could reasonably respond "WHAT?", the line fails.

ONE-THOUGHT TEST: read the approved stem plus the new ending together. It should feel like ONE PERSON finishing ONE THOUGHT. If the second half feels like a new argument, a new ad angle, supporting evidence, a history lesson, or an explanation bolted on, it is off track.

# V2 ADDENDUM 2 (Ryan verbatim, 2026-08-17): THE STOP RULE + SEQUENCE MATTERS

THE STOP RULE: do not assume an approved hook needs another sentence just because Ryan previously asked for a "second half." Before writing anything after an approved line, ask: is the hook already complete and stronger if I stop here? If yes, STOP. Do not add a sentence merely to explain it, prove it, establish credibility, fill space, complete a pattern, or satisfy an earlier request for a second half. A strong hook often creates the question and STOPS before answering or qualifying it. The BODY then handles: how the speaker knows, why she is telling us, supporting evidence, context, the story. Do not force those jobs into the hook if doing so makes the hook weaker.

SEQUENCE MATTERS: not every requirement belongs in the same sentence. "How does she know this?" means the VIDEO answers it early enough to be believed, not that the answer attaches to the hook. The sequence: 1) HOOK: make me stop and care. 2) NEXT LINE: make me believe this person has a reason to know. 3) BODY: build story/proof, deepen curiosity. 4) CLICK: leave the correct reveal for the next page. Judge each line by the job it does at THAT moment. Do not make one sentence perform the entire ad.
