# The First Batch: Bankers, Bridges, Bets

> Distilled from "THE FIRST BATCH: How to Win With a New Client" (Luke). Full source: `strategist-references/source/how-to-get-winners-first-batch.pdf`. Added 2026-08-03. Read this before speccing any batch.

## The two games

You are always playing two at once.

- **Strategy.** Can you actually find winners? Do you understand the account, the market, the gaps?
- **Trust.** You are a stranger. Every deliverable, message and ad is read as evidence about you.

They pull against each other. Strategy rewards bold moves, which is where the massive winners live. Trust punishes bold moves at first, because bold moves take time and time is exactly what a new relationship has not given you yet.

> Win reliably enough to earn trust. Spend that trust on bigger and bigger swings. Repeat.

No single move wins both games. The mix does. That is the entire reason to run a portfolio instead of a strategy.

## Step 1: The read

**Patterns are what's working.** Sort by spend, filter to active, dissect the top performers. Look in two places: messaging (what concepts and angles keep showing up, which awareness levels are hitting) and creative (what styles and formats carry the spend). One winner is an anecdote, three sharing a trait is a pattern. Write the pattern down in plain language, because that sentence is worth more than any dashboard.

**Gaps are what's missing**, and they live in predictable places: segments and personas nobody is talking to; styles and formats (if the account is all video, statics are a gap); and awareness levels, where most accounts cluster in the comfortable middle and leave true unaware at the top and creative most-aware at the bottom empty.

**Weigh the gaps with chad logic.** Not every gap is an opportunity; there has to be demand on the other side. How big is the gap, how big is the prize, how urgent is it, is it genuinely novel or just novel to this account, is it plausibly connected to what already works? There is no formula. The framework tells you where to look; taste tells you what matters.

**Then read the client.** What are they hungry for, winners or creativity? How much patience do they have? What is the relationship context? How sophisticated are they? You are forming one gut read: how much risk can this relationship absorb right now.

## Step 2: Over-deliver by count

If they hire you for 10, make 13. This does three jobs at once: it demonstrates over-delivery in the most countable way, it buys more shots on goal in a probabilistic game, and it gives the risky ideas a free home. **The ads they paid for skew safe. The surplus carries the swings.** A surplus miss cost them nothing they ordered; a surplus hit is a winner from work they never paid for, which is the strongest trust moment available.

## Step 3: The menu

Difficulty is 1 to 10 and measures **difficulty of getting a win, not value**. Some easy moves demonstrate almost no skill; some hard moves are worth taking precisely because of what they demonstrate. Track both.

### BANKERS (the sure things)
- **Style/format swap on the top spender · 1–2.** The single highest-probability play in the game. Take the script scaling hardest and change nothing but the format. The message is already proven with real dollars and the algorithm reads a new format as a genuinely new ad, so you get exploit-level reliability with explore-level reach. Best when the account has a creative gap. Rule: don't just copy, add a couple of fresh hooks so reformatting becomes craft.
- **Segment/persona transplant · 2–3.** Proven message logic aimed at a segment the account ignores. Only matters if the gap is real; a transplant into a sliver is busywork dressed as strategy.
- **Competitor swipe · 2–3.** Borrowed reliability. Be honest that it is a reliability play, not a skill demonstration. Adapt, never clone, and never let swipes be the story of the batch.

### BRIDGES (the chef's picks)
- **Concept/angle remix · 3–4.** Where you graduate from copying success to understanding it. Hold the winner, spin the rest. See the remix rule in `concepts-and-angles.md`.
- **Organic swipe · 3–4.** High-signal organic instead of paid. Softer signal, more judgment, but you catch waves before competitors.
- **Bottom of funnel · 4–5.** Quietly big spenders, cheap to make, but the ad space is narrow and they demonstrate almost no skill. Smart play is to pair BOF with a format swap so an unglamorous move gets a strategic reason to exist.
- **Internal vectors · 5–6.** The most underrated item on the menu. The vector is the common pattern running underneath ALL the winners, not any single ad. Look at eight top performers and extract the through-line ("every winner gives the buyer permission to stop blaming themselves, the villain is always external"), then express that vector in a net-new ad. Any freelancer can swap a creator; almost nobody can name the vector. Present the pattern analysis alongside the ad: it is a demonstration of how you think, disguised as an ad.

### BETS (the moonshots)
- **True net-new / unaware ads · 6–8.** Concepts the account has never touched. Most miss, and you never pretend otherwise. They matter because the hits are disproportionate (a net-new winner opens a new front rather than adding a good ad), they are the ultimate demonstration of raw creativity, and even a miss buys information if it was framed as a hypothesis. Point them at the biggest gaps from the read; one great unaware ad at the largest untouched segment beats three scattered swings. Order 2–3 per batch, no more.

**A good batch orders from all three sections. Never order only bankers.**

## Step 4: Set the dial

One dial, three positions, set by the client read. On a 13-ad batch:

| Position | When | Bankers | Bridges | Bets |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conservative | impatient, winner-hungry, burned before | 8 | 3 | 2 |
| Medium | the default when going in blind | 6–7 | 4 | 2–3 |
| Aggressive | patient, creativity-hungry, gets the game | 5 | 4 | 4 |

The exact numbers do not matter. The principle does: **the less trust and patience you have, the more Bankers you run.** You can always shift toward Bets in batch two. You cannot un-lose a client's confidence in batch one. The dial only ever moves as fast as trust.

## Step 5: Run it, narrate it

People give far more patience if they *know* you have a strategy, so a strategy kept in your head is worthless.

- **Tell the story before the results exist.** What you found, the patterns, the biggest gaps, how you split the batch and what each piece is for. Set expectations out loud: some of this is built to win fast, some may take longer or miss entirely, and that is by design.
- **Attach a one-line hypothesis to every ad**: what it is, why it should work, what you learn if it does not. Twenty minutes, and the highest-leverage twenty minutes in the framework, because a winner becomes a *predicted* winner and a loser becomes a documented learning. Once hypotheses are written down you cannot lose the demonstration game.
- **Launch Bankers first** so a win reaches the scoreboard while the Bets are still spending through learning. Overcommunicate on a cadence; never go silent long enough for them to wonder.

## Step 6: Read the signal

- **Format swap hits big** → messages were fine, creative was saturated. Run every proven message through the format gaps.
- **Segment transplant hits** → you validated a new segment, not just an ad. Treat it as a fresh mini-account with its own concepts, angles, formats and awareness levels. One transplant winner can seed an entire batch.
- **Remixes hit but swaps are flat** → inverted signal. The market has messaging headroom and the existing creative is tapped. Shift weight to Bridges, internal vectors and message-driven plays.
- **A Bet hits** → jackpot. New territory plus a massive trust deposit. Shift a full notch toward aggressive and immediately run banker-style iterations on the new winner. Your explore becomes your new exploit.
- **Nothing hits** → the moment the hypothesis layer saves you. Do not hide, lead: here is what each miss tells us, here is precisely what batch two changes and why. Losses plus a legible learning loop read as competence. Losses plus silence read as failure.

## The loop

Batch two is not a fresh start, it is the same loop with better inputs. Winners feed the Bankers, learnings feed the Read, trust feeds the dial. After three or four cycles you are the person holding the accumulated map of everything the account has learned, and that context cannot be transferred to whoever is cheaper.
