## The stack, in the order they run it

1. **Script written for the mouth.** 150 to 180 wpm, about 15 words per 5 s shot on Kling. Long lines get squeezed and read robotic.
2. **Character once, reused everywhere.** One iPhone-real still (GPT Image 2 / 2.5, Nano Banana Pro / 2, 0xROAS: Higgsfield Soul for the face then GPT image-to-image into scenes). Prompt words: "raw unedited smartphone photo, iPhone 24mm, front camera, no background blur, soft window daylight, visible pores, no beauty retouching, no dramatic grading, chest-up 9:16 at eye level." A three-panel sheet with ONE visible face; many-pose grids fail in close-ups.
3. **Motion from a real video, not invented.** A real winning UGC video is Video1, "the sole editing master" (0xROAS 10-01): framing, camera, gestures, timing, lip movement, cuts. The character sheet is Image1. Prompt: "Reference Image1 for the character only. Replace the [person] in Video1 with the character in Image1, make sure to keep everything else the same. also make sure the color grading is correct and matches the original video." Each reference gets one job. Strip burned-in captions from the master first (they survive the swap). Downscale the master to 720x1280 (Kie's pixel cap). Seedance 2.5, `duration: -1`, `aspect_ratio: adaptive`, up to 30 s per run, about $5.70 per 15 s at 720p (input + output seconds billed). The cheap yapper route is Omni 1.1 Flash on Google Flow / Kie, 4 to 10 s clips from a real screenshot with the person swapped in Nano Banana ("extend this video and make her say this..."), $0.63 to $0.84 a clip, several takes per segment, stitched in CapCut.
4. **Voice made first, by a voice tool, and the real file goes back over the picture.** ElevenLabs v4 leads the blind listening arena (ELO 1334, 11-03); Qwen-Audio 3.1, Cartesia Sonic 3.6, Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS, Fish Drama 3 follow; MiniMax Speech 2.8 and OpenAI voices are outside the top 20. The voice goes into Seedance as the audio reference so the lips follow its rhythm, BUT Seedance re-records the reference (re-timed, about 5% higher pitch, a noise bed, 32 kHz): that is the phone-call sound. So the model's audio is a scratch track and the original voice file is laid back over the finished picture (Mike Futia's skill does exactly this). The other route is ElevenLabs speech-to-speech on the video model's own audio: same timing, full-band voice, picture untouched. Everyone names voice as "the only bottleneck" and warns it drifts between clips; the fix is one locked voice file, never a fresh cast per clip.
5. **Pick takes, then repair mouths.** About 2 clips generated per usable clip (Invideo: 103 made, 49 used). Known tells: Seedance 2.5 mangles some syllables and product names; Kling 3.0 lip sync slips after about 8 s; Omni Flash stutters on about 15% of takes; Veo 3.1's mouth "doesn't look human". Sync 3 / Sync 2 Pro is the mouth repair tool. A failed section is redone "from the nearest real cut", never patched mid-shot.
6. **Finish like an editor.** CapCut: own captions, product B-roll, cuts at sentence ends, no music or a quiet bed with room tone under it, match the master's grade instead of adding a look. Pauses tightened to about 0.35 s but never digital silence. Light touch on the voice: no heavy EQ, compression or noise removal (it reads more fake). Loudness last, -14 LUFS. Topaz upscale ONCE at the end. Claude (Opus 5.5) runs the pipeline for 0xROAS, Whiskeyatx, Lachezar and neverfilmed; Kie credits run $4.90 to $7.70 per finished minute plus human review.
7. **Time and money per finished ad:** 2 to 3 hours and $73 to $145 (Invideo's measurement); a 2-minute Seedance swap about $46 a pass, $90 to $140 with 2 to 3 takes per chunk. Their best editor spends 10 to 15 hours on one video.
