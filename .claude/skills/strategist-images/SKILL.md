# Strategist Images: the ad's own line, made visible, in one second

You take a finished ad, page, or locked hook and make the static images for it. You hit Kie.ai directly; you are not exodus. Every rule below was paid for in a live grading session, most of them more than once. Read it end to end before round 1 of any campaign. The craft section is the skill; the format library (`formats.md`) is a menu of media it can wear.

The bar, Ryan, 2026-10-19: "all the images we make should be 10 out of 10s, and I shouldn't even have to give feedback... the thing that sets us apart is that not only do we create the most incredible copy, but the most incredible images and the most incredible ideas out of anyone... there should be no room for mediocrity. I really want you to be a perfectionist, like obsessive." And 2026-10-29: "You really want to be a genius with thumb-stopping, right?"

Judge every frame as him: the overstimulated strategist who has seen every ad in the market. The test is not a checklist. The test is: does a jaded stranger stop, does she click, does the page then sell. (Ryan, 2026-10-29: "Take in what I'm telling you as if missing one word would be very dangerous.")

## HOW TO READ THIS FILE (Ryan, 2026-11-01: "my only concern when you add rules is will it limit us... I just wanna make sure it doesn't put us in a box")

There are two kinds of lines in here, and they do different jobs.

**Hard rules.** The short list of things he has said never. These cut a frame before it renders:
- On a product-blind page the product never appears in any form (not blurred, not pixelated, not in a hand), and a hidden thing keeps no silhouette.
- No stock-photo people as the concept ("get rid of those completely and not ever do that again"); every person photo goes through the realism stack.
- Never reuse a frame he has not approved, and a new campaign never starts from old rounds or old picks ("a big no-no").
- Never halt the loop to ask which frame he liked; never file to Dropbox, a Doc or ClickUp before his pick.
- A video thumbnail is never a before/after pair. Compare statics are never healthy vs junk.
- Nothing invented: every frame is a line from this ad or this research, and the offer's own red lines (OFFER.md: no needle on Olavita, no dead dog on a client-visible ProDenta page, no disease words) hold.
- gpt-image-2 only; never nano-banana-pro, never the exodus Native engine.

**Taste.** Everything else: the keeper record, the "has died" lists, the craft laws, the grade lexicon. These are what has worked and what has died so far, written down so the judgment is his and not a fresh guess every round. They are how to look at a frame, not a filter that removes an idea before it is tried. Ryan, 2026-09-09, asked whether to turn a finding into a skill rule: "If that's going to limit us with images, then I don't want to do it." And 2026-10-29: "that's not necessarily a rule... I am attached to filters, zoom out, I am thinking too small." So: when the best idea for THIS ad breaks a taste line, render it, and say in the delivery which line it breaks and why it is worth it. When a round is dying, the taste lines are the first place to look for why. Never answer one of his notes by writing a new line in here without his sign-off.

## PART 1. WHAT A 10/10 LOOKS LIKE (his keeper record, every offer)

The frames he has kept, across every offer, share one grammar:

**The campaign's own claim or fear, made literal as ONE wrong or impossible physical thing, inside a picture she already recognises, shot like a real phone photo, with the subject plainly visible, readable in one second, and nothing else competing for the eye. The only question left is why. The click answers it.**

**What the agency's money says (study 2026-10-29, every Meta account, image ads only, $10.3M this year, `offers/agency/perf/2026-10-29-winning-images/FINDINGS.md`).** The top 48 by spend fall into four shapes:
1. Her worst outcome, shot literally (16): the grave, the headstone, the diapers, the nursing-home hallway, the skeleton in the hospital bed.
2. A famous brand she already owns, in a hand (11): Dawn, Coffee-mate, Kirkland water, CeraVe vs Olay outside Trader Joe's, Coke poured on grey hair.
3. Her body, real phone before/after or at its worst (11): cellulite legs, ponytail MARCH / AUGUST, a cropped face pair.
4. A handwritten line on a whiteboard or on skin (4).
None show our product. One diagram in 48. Almost none carry typed text. Each reads in one look at thumbnail size. The four shapes are where the money has gone, not a fence: a round built only inside them is nine rounds of Olavita all over again. Say which shape each frame lives in, and keep frames in the round that live outside them on purpose.

**What has died, so far (graded dead ten times or more; taste, not a ban; if one of these is the best idea for this ad, make it and say why):** a person reacting (crying, gasping, pointing) as the subject; a sketch or diagram explaining; a metaphor or symbol to decode (gauge, oven knob, hourglass, leash, scarecrow, vault door); a still object with a note on it; a quiet tasteful aftermath; a product lineup or a food hero; a comedy prop about the ad (a candle named after the breath, a toothbrush graveyard); a staged drama scene (the wife frozen in the doorway); a crowd whose skin is the point; a format-library costume on a story or a "vicious" ask; a scene that could sit under any ad in the category ("a scared senior is any ad; a hip bone frying in Crisco is only this page").

## PART 2. THE CRAFT LAWS (how a 10/10 gets made)

### 00. THE BIG ONE (Ryan, 2026-11-01, his words): "Image I think should be VERY easy to comprehend. If you can make it both FIRE as fuck and not boring + super simple to grasp so the brain doesn't have to think too long."
Two bars at once, never one without the other. Fire: a stranger's thumb stops. Simple: she gets the whole picture before she has finished looking at it, no reading, no parsing who is who, no second step. The frames that passed both in the anesthesia rounds (my read of his grades so far): the tartar on his heart, the consent sheet as his pee pad, paws braced on the vet's door, asleep on a bed of hundreds, the marquee, the lawn sign. The frames that were fire but made the brain work: the trail cam (read a timestamp and a sheet title), the courtroom sketch (work out who is who), the sports replay (a busy frame), the museum label. The frames that were simple but not fire: the licked-clean bowl, nose to nose, the rosette. Per frame, before the prompt: say the picture in five words. If it takes more, or the five words are a caption she has to read, it is not simple yet. Then ask if the five words make a jaded stranger stop. If not, it is not fire yet. Both, or cut. And simple never means plain: the round built on this note as twelve flash photos of a dog plus one prop got "crap, kind of basic again, and only 1 format" (2026-11-01, r7). His notes are cumulative. Fire, simple, AND the costume variety he asked for the round before, all at once; a round that satisfies the newest note and drops the last two is a regression.

### 0. The thumb stop comes first (Ryan, 2026-11-01: "If I'm scrolling with my thumb, it's going to stop me no matter what." 2026-10-29: "You really want to be a genius with thumb-stopping, right?")
The first question for every frame, before the line, before the format: what is the one thing in this picture a stranger physically stops on? In his keeps that thing is almost always one impossible or violent physical event in an otherwise ordinary photo: termites pouring out of the creamer into a cut-open bone, bone chunks as the ice in her Diet Coke, the statin bottle burning at her table, a liver sunk in a glass of Coke, a dog's breath glowing like lava. His old words for the bar: "FUCK tons of emotion and/or curiosity", "holy shit". So the impossible-thing lane is the DEFAULT door for an ad static, and most of a round lives there. Readable paperwork (a stamp, a clipboard, a card, a board) is a real lane too and has sold, but a round that is mostly paperwork reads "good, not wow, kind of random" (anesthesia r1, 2026-11-01: eleven frames, one lava breath, the rest documents). Lead with the wow; let two or three readable anchors ride along. And the wow has to be the page's own cause or effect made physical (the tartar on his heart), not a visual pun on a word in the page (teeth that glow because they are "white"): the first read, the second "doesn't make any sense." And his standing note on every rule in here, said again the same day: "do not limit us." This is the first door, not the only one; when a frame outside this lane is the best idea for the ad, make it.

### 1. Congruent means intentional: a stranger could point to the line
Every frame IS a line from this ad or this page (the headline, a list card's claim, the hook, a beat of the story), quoted in the README before the prompt exists. Ryan, 2026-10-30, on seven flash candids of a scared senior for the OsteoShield page: "not congruent with the Lander, just basic images of stuff, I don't really know what I'm looking at." Fixed by rendering one page line per frame as one impossible thing (bone chunks as ice in the Diet Coke, the bone frying in oil, her moth-eaten jaw through her cheek). "Random", "unintentional" and "not related to the story" all mean the same thing: no line under the frame. Say which line each frame is in the delivery.

**Intentional, not random (Ryan, 2026-11-01, r8: "a lot of these are just random for the sake of being random vs intentional as fuck while still following all feedback").** The costume never comes first. When a frame starts from a costume (a parade balloon, a roadside giant, a bronze statue, a Walk of Fame star) and the page line gets bent to fit it, he reads random. Order of work: the page's line or her real research line first, the one thing that makes it land second, the costume last and only when it makes the line land harder than a plain photo would (the departures board makes CANCELLED land; the propaganda poster makes NO ANESTHESIA a cry). Write the intent in one sentence per frame: "this frame exists because the page says X and she feels Y." If the sentence is about the costume, cut it.

### 2. Curiosity is not confusion
Ryan, 2026-10-30: "If I looked at this image, I would just be so dying to know what's going on, instead of being confused. Confusion is different from curiosity." Curiosity = she knows exactly what she is looking at (a vet's trash can, her kitchen bowl, a lab sheet, a dog at the storm door) and ONE thing in it is wrong in a way she cannot explain. Confusion = she has to ask "what is that?" (a withheld object, an abstract pinch of nothing, a cap floating above a tooth, a root growing to the heart). Per frame, before the prompt, write (a) what a stranger says she sees in one second, in plain words, and (b) the one question she asks. If (a) takes more than a sentence, or (b) is "what is that?", cut the frame. Same law in his words on other days: "if you can't explain this image to me in five seconds, it's probably too confusing"; "usually people look at the image first and then they read the copy"; "they can't be confusing as fuck."

### 3. The subject must be visible and the category must be named
"If I was just looking at this image and nothing else, I would have absolutely no idea that this is related to cholesterol at all" (Akka, 2026-10-30). A static for a cholesterol page has cholesterol in the picture: a lab sheet reading Total Cholesterol with the number, a pharmacy label reading ATORVASTATIN, the plaque-caked artery. A bone page shows bone. A teeth page shows teeth or the tartar. "No text" never meant removing the anchor word, and it never meant removing the labels that ARE the proof (ON THE STATIN / OFF THE STATIN; JAN / JUL; SHE'S 91 / I'M 68). It means no captions, slogans, or lines that explain.

### 4. Weird = a familiar picture with the ad's swap, one idea per frame
Ryan's own example (Akka, 2026-10-29): cooked eggs resting over her eyes like spa cucumber slices while she calmly lights the statin bottle on fire at her table. "I wanted you to think through why I did that, kind of like a weirdness thing, instead of just doing a bunch of random egg shit." The why: a picture everyone recognises in a second (the spa face) with the ad's objects swapped in, so the swap carries the claim (eggs are her comfort, the statin is what she burns). Rules that followed from his grades on rounds 8 to 11:
- Start from pictures she already knows (the spa face, grace before dinner, birthday candles, the ring box, the WORLD'S BEST GRANDMA mug, breakfast in bed, the ambulance stretcher) and swap the ad's objects in.
- The wrong thing is the AD'S OWN FACT one step from real (a hospital wristband because she was booked for anesthesia; NO FOOD AFTER MIDNIGHT taped to the empty bowl; a $1,200 tag because that was the quote; Max in a hospital gown because he went under). A comedy prop ABOUT the ad (a candle named after the breath, a kissing booth) needs a second step and dies as "random".
- ONE idea from the ad per frame, each weird thing stating its idea without words (thumbs-up on the stretcher = relief; VERY COMMON stamped on her forehead = the brush-off; a plumber pulling pills out of her drain = the clogged exit). Nine frames of eggs with fuzzy meanings = "one idea repeated badly... you're not thinking, you're just repeating."
- "Less confusing" = the weird thing big, centered, obvious; everything else plain.
- Render one or two of HIS examples as he described them, then the rest have to be yours. "Don't copy that... you'll have to think for yourself."
- The villain is the whole bottle, not a pill (his correction on the eggs frame).

### 5. Vicious = her ugliest true fear, shown flat, not a costume
Ryan, 2026-10-29: "They need to be so much more fucking vicious." What he means is never a costume (crayon, vintage ad, whiteboard, receipt, phone UI) and never a pile of insults ("mean is not vicious"). Vicious is the ugliest TRUE thing the research says she is afraid of, shown flat in her own house, or the villain done violence: the 14-slot pill organizer like her mother's, the commode by the bed, the velcro blouse, the car keys handed over, the briefs on her dresser, the bone crushed to dust over her eggs, the statin bottle on fire, half her face drooped. His own examples: "A close-up of a face with half of it droopy... Something with the toxins in it, like a mix, some kind of thing with the soda... what does a statin look like? Maybe lighting a statin on fire. You see how I'm thinking? Keep thinking like that." On a "vicious" ask: open the buyer brief's fears and lead with THOSE, phone-real, text only when the object itself prints it. Pairs, cards and library costumes have read as "pulling from other images" on every vicious ask so far; if one is the hardest version of her fear, use it, and say so.

### 6. The villain can be a person
On a hook aimed at a person (the doctor, the vet, the groomer), put that person in the frame getting what the hook says: the doctor with his head in his hands over her lab sheet; her hand flipping him off across his desk, POV; claymation of her shoving the bottle down his pants. Three of the five Akka shove-the-statin keeps (15 rounds, 171 frames) were this. The villain is a person, not a prop.

### 7. Feel something, through the subject, not through a face acting
Ryan, 2026-10-29: "I don't feel anything when I'm looking at these images. They don't pull at the heartstrings... I don't feel connected to the essence of the story." And the next day, when the fix was faces: "a scared senior is any ad." The feeling comes from a true thing shown, carried by the campaign's subject: on a pet offer the DOG carries it (the surviving dog with the dead one's collar, the dog at the storm door at night, the dog licking her tears); on a bone offer the bone; on a skin offer her own fold; on a liver offer her eye at 3:14. In every keep so far the person is the carrier of the object (her hands, her thumb, her foot on the cracking carpet), and a face as the subject has died (the vet crying, the leash tug, the doorway); that is the pattern, not a ban on faces. A reaction scene only reads when the hook line sits under it, so it stays in ads with copy and never on a hero. Before the frame exists, name the feeling a stranger has in one second (ache, envy, relief, shame, joy) and the beat it comes from, quoted. Payoff frames are allowed and he asks for them ("fear in the words, payoff in the picture"; the dream-scenario frame he requested on TPS): the change has to be visible in one second (the dog's white grin, one 65-year-old face that reads 45 among faces that read 70, the smooth face behind the jar of sludge). A posed glowing woman with nothing to compare her to is stock.

### 8. Loud, but the loud thing is the wrong thing, not drama
Quiet = boring: a hand close-up, a soft pensive moment, a calm cut-open bone in a hand, a still object on a table, all dead ("look how vanilla your images are"). But staged drama is dead too: the wife in the doorway, the rival on her knees, the fall, the family meeting ("PUSSY ASS IMAGES... a movie still, not proof"; "I have no idea what I'm looking at"). The voltage lives in the impossible or violent thing itself happening TO HER BODY OR HER HOUSE in the act of the ad beat (her foot on carpet cracking like ice, the chair with thigh-bone legs snapping under her, calcium pills pouring straight through a hollow bone, the dog awake in the dentist chair), not in a prop on a table (the hourglass, the deli slicer, the wall of calendars: "boring af confusing too") and not in a person emoting. Metaphors have needed decoding (confusing) and carried no stakes (boring) every time so far; a metaphor that reads in one second with the category visible is a different thing and is welcome.

### 9. Realism: it has to pass as a photo a real person took
"If it looks like it cost money, it dies." AI-photoreal faces read AI to him no matter the engine. The stack that passes (REA r8, confirmed on Cloud9 and Olavita):
- People come from z-image with the family-member flash spec (three feet away, flash on, head and shoulders front-on, crooked framing, real uneven skin, no makeup, no phone in frame, each person described one by one: age, build, hair, race, AND CLOTHING named every time or she renders bare-shouldered). Under 800 characters; never end the prompt with a bare format word (it leaks into signs).
- The change (younger, smoother, the gap) is a gpt-image-2 image-to-image edit of that same frame ("recreate exactly, change only..."), never a fresh text-to-image portrait. A text-to-image "64-year-old woman" reads as a different woman and gets the round thrown out.
- Both halves of anything degraded the same way (2014-iPhone JPEG blockiness, colour noise, warm cast); the after told NOT brightened, NO glow, visible pores, same old phone. Clean renders at full resolution read AI no matter the crop.
- Stock = posed, lined up in a row, evenly lit, golden hour, bokeh, toothy laugh at a party, rooftops, boats, weddings, pools, the same clothes or hair on two women, a held print that copies the live photo. Real = crooked, cluttered, mid-moment, overhead kitchen light, a plain room, eyes into the lens, a small smile.
- Drawn and handmade formats never claim to be photos, so they never read fake; the seam between a photo and an illustrated window always does (one committed medium end to end).
- Hero slots render crisp. The feed-candid blur and flash kills a hero ("blurry").
- Gore reads AI, anatomy reads real (teeth: correct canine shapes, a yellow-brown layer thickest at the gum line, no blood). Research what the real thing looks like before rendering it (the tartar that comes off is a flat chalky crescent, tan to grey-green, fingernail size, never a bag of it).

### 10. Text: a treatment is a format, the line is a headline, half the round has none
- The default is no text, or the line INSIDE the picture on a real thing (marker on tape, a sticky note, a whiteboard, the lab sheet's own red pen, a speech bubble in a comic, a period headline in a vintage ad, a kid's crayon lettering). A composited bar is one frame per round at most, never the house style. "Why is it the same format every time with that white text underneath?" (graded three times on Akka alone).
- Count text treatments before firing; reject the list if any treatment appears twice. Half the round carries no text at all.
- When there is a line, it is a standalone headline: a cold stranger who read nothing else knows who it is for, the thing, and what she gets. "The #1 cream" fails; "The cream dermatologists ranked #1 of 2026" passes. One idea, 6 to 10 words, two rows max, no coloured word, no second sentence, no doctor set-up, the ugliest true noun the page actually pays off. Fragments and aftermath lines that need the ad ("Cut everything they said. Still 216.") are "random AF".
- Hand-built UI (Notes app, iMessage, Facebook thread, error dialog) is HTML flattened with headless Chrome, never AI (AI cannot fake OS chrome). Red circles, arrows and scrawls are drawn in PIL on a clean render.
- Labels that are the proof (dates, ages, numbers on two sheets) are not "text" and are never cut on a "no text" note.

### 11. Busy = more than one thing to look at
Cap every frame at two elements (the subject huge, plus one device or none; a line counts as an element). "More curiosity" and "more social proof" are added INSIDE the one thing (the one jar with its label scratched off on an ordinary shelf; one 65-year-old face that reads 45 filling the frame), never by adding things. Crowds, signs, corkboards of photos and cluttered counters got "much worse, too busy, messy, boring, you went backwards." Busy and boring are the same failure: nothing to land on. Social proof has only read as the same face twice, one reaction to her face, or one line that says the proof; a choir of nice-skinned women was "I have no idea what I'm looking at." A group can still work when one thing in it is the point (the bingo-night sign, the two littermates with the X).

### 12. Before/after pairs: one recipe, big gap, different world
Every pair on every offer goes through `offers/tps/images/lander-plain-ba/pairs.py` (or `tools/statics/kie.py` pair helpers), never a new method. BEFORE: z-image flash candid, head and shoulders. AFTER: gpt-image-2 image-to-image from that before with the HEAD/TAIL prompt verbatim: a completely DIFFERENT photo of the SAME person weeks later, new place, new clothes, new hair, daylight, different angle, "do not reuse the room, outfit or pose". Only the person and the angle's prop carry over; the world cannot ("same exact background, same clothing... AI again"). The gap is twenty years plus joy (a hard 70-75 before, a styled laughing 48 after), or visibly ILL in the pain scene to visibly THRIVING in an ordinary place; a subtle change is "the same woman, fake." The angle's object sits in both halves (the glass and the 3:14 clock; the salt shaker; the lab sheet by her cheek); a clean skin pair only fits an offer whose angle IS the face. Page pairs carry labels only (DAY 1 / DAY 30), no caption line. One AI render holding both halves reads as one photo; two independent renders drift; the i2i-from-the-before is the only shape he has kept. On teeth: render the before, make the after by i2i, same teeth, different day. The TPS lipstick-ad colour-box treatment (same crop both halves) is for the TPS HERO slot only.

### 13. Compare statics are a quiz between rival brands
Both panels are things she buys and already has a side on: two brands of the same thing (Coffee-mate vs International Delight, Diet Coke vs Diet Pepsi, Advil vs Tylenol) or the same brand then vs now (her mother's 6½ oz Sprite vs today's 20 oz). One branded package per panel, huge, held up close, on a plain everyday background that contrasts with the item and differs per side. The line is a question ("Is Coke or Pepsi shrinking your penis?" / "Which painkiller is draining your testosterone?"), never a statement, never a stat or a doctor. Never healthy vs junk ("that's stupid"), never brand vs generic ("why would we do a sugar bowl instead of the brand of sugar"), never a label variant, never a consequence in a panel (a creamer vs a bedpan is a poster, not a quiz), and every item as common as Sprite for THIS buyer, proven from a diet study for that buyer, both sides research-based. When only the text is wrong, re-overlay the same photos; never re-render.

### 14. The slot decides the rules
- **Ad static:** sells the click to the next page; a line is allowed (standalone headline) but half the round has none.
- **Lander hero:** one page line as one impossible thing in a real photo, crisp, no text (or the proof labels). Open the offer's live `landers/*/image1.jpg` strip first and match that register ("this not the winning hero image for TPS"). On TPS the hero is the control hero's treatment on the new feature, not a new idea. On OsteoShield the keeper shape is a labelled comparison or a circled scan with the ad's words. Heroes never carry a feed-candid blur.
- **Video thumbnail:** relevant to the video, curiosity plus what's in it for her, never a before/after pair, never a caption, never the product. The one that landed on ProDenta: the mystery thing (flakes in the bowl) plus the payoff (white teeth) in ONE frame plus one red mark.
- **Product-blind funnel (every VSL page):** the product never appears in any form, not blurred, not pixelated, not a tub in a vet's hand ("we would never show a product because we don't want to tell them we're selling them a product"). A hidden thing has no silhouette (pixelate a region of a scan, never an object that keeps its capsule shape). Never a competitor's product-page ad as the model.
- **Proof pair under the Watch button:** law 12.
- **A like on one slot is never the other slot's brief** ("I liked it for the hero, not the thumbnail").

### 15. The format library is a wardrobe, not the idea
`formats.md` is where media live (neon, crayon, claymation, anatomy plate, whiteboard, 1950s ad, comic, and the Atria harvests). Use it:
- When the idea needs a surface that carries a headline (SCR culture angles kept 8 of 60, seven of them published-media formats: tabloid, 1960s poster, TV lower-third, comic strip).
- When the photo lane is blocked (sex offers: positions live in watercolor and the sex-manual pencil plate).
- For medium swaps on a winner, picking media that transform the surface completely (neon, engraved plate, woodcut, flat comic, oil), never two paper-sketch media in one round.
- Blocks verbatim, a different block per frame, never your go-to eight (crayon, 1950s, comic, neon, marker portrait, circled candid, clay, Snapchat): "there are more formats than this, you tend to always go to the same ones."
Where it has not worked (taste, not a ban):
- As the answer to "vicious", "weird", "feel something", or a story ad. A costume has read as "pulling from other images" however new the words on it were, and illustration on the ProDenta story ads was "vanilla, I'm sick of the same animated images." The exception he named himself: claymation of her shoving the bottle down the doctor's pants was a keep, because the idea was the frame and the clay only let it render.
- As a variety engine. Nine formats of the same claim is "a lot of repetition with the formats... I don't feel anything." A format carries no feeling on its own; it is chosen for the feeling, and two frames in one format is fine when each is a different beat.
- Rejected formats stay rejected (the REJECTED line at the bottom of formats.md).
- The library's oldest blocks are OVERDONE (Ryan, 2026-11-01, on a round of clay, crayon, 1950s ad, comic split, stick-figure doors, billboard, TV news, thermal, anatomy plate: "these formats are over done, let's try others. A "more formats" note means NEW costumes (trail cam, courtroom sketch, nature documentary subtitle, sports replay telestrator, a tattoo, a lawn sign, sidewalk chalk, a museum specimen case, a cave painting, a theater marquee) and the IDEAS stay on the lane the chat already locked. Reaching for the library's stock moves (the queue at two doors, the housewife, the old masters) reads as pulling from old ads.

### 16. On-the-nose and gross offers show the literal thing
On an audience test or an on-the-nose campaign the literal thing (the bone, the liver, the prostate, the skin) is the centre of every frame, aftermath scenes saved for the angle campaigns. When the winners show the gross thing (a turd in the bowl), the next round shows it bigger and uglier; tasteful stand-ins are "the image version of writing like a pussy." A gross thing without the idea (hands in a dog's mouth, pus) is gross, not vicious; the stakes live in the idea (the anesthesia waiver, the $1,700).
