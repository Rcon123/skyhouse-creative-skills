## Concept derivation (the actual craft)

Mine the AD ITSELF for image concepts — never generic scenes:

- **No-text emotional images**: find the ad's deepest emotional beats (the moment of shame, the moment of transformation, the social payoff, the villain) and stage ONE of them per image as a scene a stranger instantly feels. Before/after is allowed but story-moments usually beat it: the stared-at woman at brunch, the jar in the trash, the husband leaning in again. Zero text. The primary text does the selling.
- **Text images**: ONE object/scene + the headline. Text must be prescriptive and specific (say exactly what the reader gets: "dermatologists ranked the top 5 creams of 2026", never coy fragments). Comic-letterer clean lettering: thick uniform caps, straight baselines, legible at thumbnail.
- **THE STANDALONE-HEADLINE RULE (Ryan, hard rule, same as his subhead skim test):** image text is a headline — a cold viewer who read NOTHING else must know exactly what's going on. Every referent must be defined inside the image's own text. "The #1 cream" FAILS (what list? #1 of what?). "The cream dermatologists ranked #1 of 2026" passes: names the thing, the authority, the frame. Read the text standalone before rendering; if any word needs outside context, rewrite.
- **DREAM-SCENARIO images (Ryan-requested type):** alongside pain peaks, render "I want to be that woman" frames — her on top of the world, SKIN as the visible star: golden light on luminous cheekbones, heads turning, envied at the table. Aspiration so strong the viewer feels the pull.
- Every image passes: one focal point, parseable in 1 second, tons of curiosity, relevant to THIS audience.
- **THE FIVE-SECOND TEST, AND THE IMAGE IS READ FIRST (Ryan approved 2026-09-09; his words from the 09-04 and 09-08 Ary reviews):** "if you can't explain this image to me in five seconds, it's probably too confusing." "usually people look at the image first and then they read the copy." "being super clear on, like, if they read the image first, would they understand what's going on?" Before rendering, write the five-second explanation for each concept. If it needs the ad copy to make sense, kill it. And: "Just make sure it's related to your ads. That's the big thing."
- **THE HOLY-SHIT BAR (Ryan, hard rule):** every image must stop the scroll at all costs — "FUCK tons of emotion and/or curiosity." Competent and pleasant = dead. Crank the voltage: public tears, jaw-on-the-floor reactions, impossible images (same age split-face), mass-movement proof (a trash can overflowing with syringes), forbidden peeks, innocent-cruelty (kid-drawing "GRANDMA WON THE WEDDING"). If a concept wouldn't make a stranger physically stop and say "holy shit," replace it before rendering.
- **THE CLARITY BAR (Ryan, hard rule, same weight as holy-shit):** "they can't be confusing as fuck." Voltage NEVER buys narrative complexity. Enforce: ONE person max (two only in an obvious before/after), NO relationships to decode (a scene with two women where you must infer who's the ex = dead), NO backstory required — the emotion must be legible from the image alone, the WHY is the click. Headline ≤ 8 words. A stranger must state what's happening in one sentence within 1 second. Holy-shit comes from ONE simple dramatic thing, never from a story you have to reconstruct.
- **THE PEAK-MOMENT RULE (Ryan, hard rule):** stage the story's MOST EXTREME cinematic peak, never the tasteful aftermath. Wrong: woman serenely smiling "she thinks I got a face-lift." Right: the wife FROZEN IN THE DOORWAY catching them in bed, purse falling mid-air; the rival ON HER KNEES in a parking lot begging for the surgeon's name. Find each beat's soap-opera maximum — biggest stakes, biggest body language, rawest emotion — and render THAT frame.
- **THE CINEMATIC-DEVICE LAYER (the final level — what separates good from "best of all time"):** on top of faithful + loud + simple, every image gets ONE movie-poster device: an INVERSION the brain must stop to resolve ("SHE'S 52. I'M 63." where the younger looks older), a MAGIC-REAL image (the mirror reflecting her younger self, glowing), an EXTREME CLOSE-UP (one face filling the frame, one tear breaking), or a POV shot (her hands grabbing YOUR arm — the viewer becomes the narrator). Flat mid-shot compositions are the enemy; pick the device before writing the prompt.
- **LOUDNESS NEVER LICENSES DRIFT OR OVERLOAD (Ryan, graded):** when cranking drama, the scene must STILL be an actual ad beat (graded fails: shattering jar, burning wedding photo — neither in the copy), text stays ONE headline + ONE short subline max (the \$15k image's three text blocks = "hard to follow"), and the scene must be physically logical (a phone can't display its photo on its back side). Best trick: use the ad's VERBATIM dialogue as the headline ("Please. Just tell me who your surgeon is.") — fidelity and context for free. Check avatar AGE matches the ad in every render.
- **QUIET = BORING (Ryan, graded on the ring/kitchen-table round):** subtle poetic devastation (a hand closeup, a soft pensive moment) is invisible at thumbnail and dies in feed. Loudness test: does the frame contain visible ACTION or SHOCK — something shattering, burning, falling, being caught, someone begging/screaming/staring — or a forbidden-scene voyeur angle? Emotional ≠ quiet. Every emotional image must ALSO be loud.
- **THE AD-FIDELITY RULE (Ryan, hard rule):** every image stages a moment that LITERALLY HAPPENS IN THE AD COPY — never invented adjacent drama. (Graded fail: rendering the girlfriend "crying at the wedding" when the ad says her jaw dropped at the purse; "grandma won the wedding" when nobody in the ad is a grandma.) Pull the scene list from the ad's actual beats verbatim before ideating. No-text images = ONE ad moment staged with huge, visibly readable emotion on the face (horror, disbelief-joy, triumph); if the emotion isn't unmistakable in the render, it failed.
- Angle diversity across the set: no two images share the same door into the ad.


## SEX-OFFER STATICS: Ryan's three laws (2026-09-08, TestoGreens wife "advice" campaign, PROVISIONAL until Ryan grades the wording)

Three rounds died before these landed ("garbage", "weak boring, doesn't turn me on", "not sure you're using the skill"):
1. **REAL TEXT BEATS DESIGNED TEXT.** The account study of the same day: every converting static carried text that physically exists in the scene (marker on the toilet lid, the note on the milk jug, index cards, a printed billboard) or no text at all. A white Impact bar composited on top is designed text and does not convert. Put the line INSIDE the photo: handwriting on paper, an index card in her hand, lipstick on the mirror, a notepad, a whiteboard, a taped note. Overlay only as a last resort when the engine refuses the baked line.
2. **HER BODY, ATTRACTIVE, EXPLICIT AS THE ENGINE ALLOWS.** "Cleavage, camel toe, sex positions, more of the woman's body, attractive women." The buyer is the man; the frame is what he is about to get. Positions live in the illustration lane (watercolor, sex-manual pencil plate) where a photo would refuse. Objects and hands "wink"; they do not arouse.
3. **CURIOSITY TIED TO THE AD, NEVER THE SAME FORMAT TWICE.** Every frame stages a beat from the ad (5am, the counter, twice a day, "not tonight") and each frame in the round uses a different physical format for the line.
