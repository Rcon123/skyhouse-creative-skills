# One-shot video

Script and idea in, `final.mp4` out. You are the director and the editor: you design the shots, the scripts in `tools/video/` do the rendering, timing and checking.

**Why this works when one-prompt video does not.** Two things kill an AI video before anyone reads the copy: a mouth that does not match the words, and a voice that sounds generated. Both are solved by where the voice comes from.

- **The voice is generated by the video model, with the picture** (`voice.mode: "native"`, the default since 2026-10-05). One line is cast as the voice anchor; every other line is generated with that clip's audio as a voice reference, so one person speaks the whole ad. The mouth is made with the voice, so sync is native. Ryan graded a text-to-speech read "horrible, super fake"; the voices that pass come out of the video models, which is also what the people he follows on X do.
- **Every clip is checked by machine before you see it:** did it say the exact words (whisper against the script), is it the same voice as the anchor (speaker embedding), does the mouth follow (SyncNet). Misses are rerolled.
- **The clean generated voice is seated in the room** the picture shows (`voice_fx.py`), and a listening judge (`voice_judge.py`, Gemini on Kie) scores the finished track, because the builder cannot hear.

The older route, one text-to-speech voiceover driving every shot (`voice.mode: "tts"`), still works and still syncs. Use it only when Ryan asks for a specific TTS voice or supplies a recorded voiceover.

## The build

Everything runs as `tools/video/.venv/bin/python tools/video/oneshot.py <step> <project>`. Projects live in `offers/<offer>/videos/<name>/` (or `output/video/<name>/` when there is no offer). Working examples: `offers/akka/videos/1955-coke-yapper-demo/` (one woman to camera, cutaways, before and after stills) and `offers/tps/videos/drama-scenes/who-is-he-pipeline-test/` (two people, singles).

### 0. Take the script as written

**Length and the loved link (dreaming 2026-11-02, sessions 11-02_10fe9d41, 11-03_311993cc).** A script is 2 minutes, 2.5 max; if the script given runs longer, say so in the first reply before any plan. When Ryan sends an Atria link with "I love this", he watched the first 15 to 20 seconds: "I only watched the first 15 to 20 seconds... I'm not attached to the whole video... 2 minutes max, 2.5 minutes max." Deliver the open first, in chat: the card line plus the first 4 to 6 spoken lines for the offer. Never a beat map of the whole ad (that was built three times for a 2:41 ad and cut off each time).

The words are Ryan's and they are graded. Never rewrite, trim or reorder them. Read the offer's `OFFER.md` if there is one (funnel type, compliance lines, what must stay blind). If the script has production notes or bracket cues ([BEFORE PHOTO]), they are instructions for the plan. If something in the script cannot be shown as written, say so in the report; do not quietly change it.

### 0b. Study the reference before anything is designed (mandatory, enforced)

Ryan, 2026-10-21, after a twin built from 22 sparse frames and a transcript ("not even remotely close... I feel nothing... you just spent money without following my directions. This cannot happen ever again"): **no paid step runs without a measured study of the swipe.** `oneshot.py frames|native|clips|voice` refuse (`SPEND GATE`) unless the project has `ref/STUDY.md` with no TODO left, `ref/cuts.txt` and `ref/strip_*.jpg`, or plan.json carries `"reference": "none"` plus `"reference_none_because"`.

1. Get the actual video file into `ref/` (the Atria CDN mp4, the reel's progressive mp4, Dropbox). A cover still or a transcript is not a reference.
2. `oneshot.py study <project>`: 1 fps strips of every 30 s, every cut time, caption frames, and a STUDY.md skeleton.
3. Fill STUDY.md by LOOKING at the strips: what is on screen each second of the first 20, where the stakes land and what picture does it, the typography (crop a caption at full size), and a shot map with a reference shot beside every planned shot. The emotion of a swipe lives in that shot structure and its cut rate, not in its surface style.
4. Voice: every generated speaker carries `voice.speakers.<name>.cast` = who picked the voice and when. A narrator is cast from a blind reel Ryan picks from (`tools/video/voicelab/`), never an anchor borrowed to skip the stop; the gate refuses an empty `cast`.
5. Report the reference numbers next to the build's numbers at hand-off (cuts in 20 s, wpm, pictures per minute).
6. **Watch the reference play**, not strips. Every shot of a twin moves unless the reference shot it mirrors is a still (`"ref_still": true`, named in STUDY.md); the gate refuses other stills. A reel-picked voice ships raw: the gate refuses `tempo` and `fx` on it.

### 1. Write plan.json (free, and where the quality is decided)

**Look for graded assets first.** Before designing a single frame, search the offer for images Ryan has already picked for this script or character: `offers/<offer>/images/**/PICKED/`, any `NOTES.md` beside them, and the memory notes for the campaign. If they exist, they ARE the masters: copy them into the project's `frames/` under the frame ids and build every other frame as an edit of them. His notes on those rounds are rules for this build (for Carol: a selfie is one arm up, standing, never sitting with both hands on a mug; a different outfit in each photo that was taken on a different day). When the script names a real branded object ("my grandmother's Coke"), show the real thing from a real reference photo. A generic stand-in makes the line meaningless.

Split the script into beats and decide the picture for every line:

- **Talking beats** of 8 to 30 words, cut at sentence ends or comma pauses. Pick the frame, alternate `zoom` 1.0 and 1.14 or change setting so no two neighbours match. **Clip length comes from the word count:** about 180 words a minute, so words / 3 seconds, rounded up (`_native_duration` in oneshot.py sizes it at words / 2.8 + 0.5). Never ask for an 8-second clip on a 12-word line; a long clip makes the model pad with repeats or a slow read. Any talking stretch that measures under 160 wpm in `check` is a miss: reroll it, never retime it. (dreaming 2026-11-02, session 10-28_47dc22db, batch 03)
- **Engine by format:** someone talking to the lens (yapper, expert, selfie) stays on the default, Wan. Scenes where people talk to each other (drama, interview) set `"talk_engine": "kling-avatar-pro"`.
- **Cutaways** on the nouns: quote the words (`on`) and show exactly that thing. One idea per shot, one action per clip, camera stated.
- **Stills** for any photo the script points at.
- **Frames:** one master per character, every other frame of them an edit of the master. Lips closed, face frontal, nothing over the chin, props already in hand.
- **Voice:** `"mode": "native"`, an engine (default `seedance2-mini`) and, per speaker, a `voice` paragraph: age, accent, texture, pace, how close the mic is, and "a person talking, not a person reading". Model it on the working example in `offers/akka/videos/1955-coke-yapper-v2/plan.json`. Ryan's winning yappers talk almost without pause at 160 to 195 words a minute; never direct a voice to slow down or leave silences.
- Spell numbers as words in `text`; use `captions.replace` for display.

Then: `oneshot.py validate <project>`. Fix every ERROR and every WARN you cannot justify. It prints the cost estimate and the Kie balance. If the balance is short, say so plainly and stop there; do not start a build that cannot finish.

### 2. Frames, then LOOK

`oneshot.py frames <project>`, then open `qa/frames_sheet.jpg` with the Read tool and run the frame checks in `references/qa.md`. Redo misses with `frames <project> --only id1,id2`. Frames are 5 cents; this is the cheapest place to be picky. Do not go on with a wrong face. A frame file that already exists in `frames/` is never regenerated, so graded images are dropped in by hand (see step 0).

### 3. Native: the voice and the talking picture in one pass

`oneshot.py native <project>` in the background. It casts each speaker's voice on one line (or uses `voice/anchor_<speaker>.wav` if Ryan already picked a voice), generates every other line with that voice locked, then checks each clip for words, voice match and sync and rerolls misses up to twice. It ends by building the voice track and timeline from the clips. Read its last lines: any id listed under NEEDS WORK is handled in step 5. Every line of the script is generated, including lines that play over B-roll (only their audio is used).

Voice casting is the one decision nothing here can make alone. If Ryan has not picked a voice for this character: run a casting reel first (`tools/video/voicelab/shootout_r1.py` + `reel.py` are the pattern: one line, several engines or several takes, lettered, blind), send it, and let him pick. A picked voice is saved as the anchor and reused across videos for that character.

### 4. B-roll clips

`oneshot.py clips <project>` in the background (in native mode this renders only cutaways and B-roll shots). Wan takes about a minute a clip. Never kill a quiet run; if one dies, rerun the same command and it resumes the same Kie tasks without paying twice.

### 5. LOOK at the clips

`oneshot.py gate <project>` builds `qa/clips_sheet.jpg` (and rescoring sync). Open the sheet and run the by-eye checks in `references/qa.md` (identity, hands, the action happened, no burned-in text, B-roll mouths closed). Redo one line with `native <project> --only <id>`, one B-roll shot with `clips <project> --only <id>`. Two rerolls per clip, one change each time, then cover it with a cutaway. After redoing any line run `native <project>` once more with no `--only` so the voice track is rebuilt.

(TTS mode instead runs `voice`, `frames`, `clips`, `gate` in that order: see `references/plan-format.md`.)

### 6. Assemble and check

`oneshot.py assemble <project>` builds the cut from the word timings, slides any clip the gate measured as offset, burns captions, mixes and exports. `oneshot.py check <project>` rescues nothing; it measures the finished file: sync on every talking stretch of the export, transcript against the script, loudness, cost. Open `qa/final_filmstrip.jpg` and read it against the script, line by line: is the thing she names on screen when she names it?

Changing the edit (zoom, cutaway timing, captions, a still's push-in) costs nothing: edit plan.json and rerun `assemble` and `check`.

### 6b. The editor's pass (before anything is sent)

You are also the chief editor. The gates measure clips; a viewer watches a cut. Before a file leaves the project:

1. `tools/video/.venv/bin/python tools/video/edit_review.py <project>`: bed continuity (a music or ambience bed that drops at a cut), motion spikes in generated shots (a bounce, a nod, a whip that is not in the script), and the shot map. Anything under NEEDS A LOOK is fixed first.
2. Watch every generated clip as its 6-frame strip (`qa/<id>.sheet.jpg`) and ask one question per clip: is anything happening here that the original or the script did not ask for? Motion prompts invent business ("bounces on her toes", "nods hard twice"); people in real ads mostly stand still.
3. When the build is a twin of an existing ad, read the shot map against the original's timeline shot for shot: same picture type in the same place (photo, two-shot, single, listener), same durations within a second. A talking shot that sits where the original had a listener is a structure change, not a detail.
4. REVIEW on an on-screen talking clip ("mouth moving in silence") is a miss for the viewer even when the number passes. `voice.strict_sync: true` makes the native step reroll it.
5. Read the final filmstrip against the script line by line, then the export sync numbers from `check`. Only then hand off.

### 7. Hand off

Send `final.mp4` with SendUserFile and report in plain words:

- where the file is, its length and what it cost (from `qa/final_report.json`)
- the gate numbers: how many talking shots passed first time, what was rerolled and why
- anything you could not verify by measurement (you cannot hear: voice character and music taste are Ryan's call) and anything in the script you could not show as written
- one line on what you would change first if he wants another pass

No Google Doc, no ClickUp, no Dropbox unless he asks (his standing rule: drafts stay local until graded). Append what this build taught you to `references/lessons.md`.

## Standing rules

- **Do not stop to ask.** Ryan's rule for render loops: diagnose the miss, fix it, render the next take. The only hard stops are a Kie balance that cannot cover the build and a script that asks for something this skill will not make (below).
- **Measure, then say.** A step is done when the sheet has been looked at and the numbers are in range. Never report "lip sync is perfect"; report the scores. A check you skipped is reported as skipped.
- **One voice, never retimed.** No `atempo` on clips, no re-voicing after picture, no speech words in B-roll prompts.
- **Never call a voice good.** You cannot hear it. Report the judge's scores and the voice-match numbers, say plainly that the voice is unjudged until Ryan has listened, and give him a reel to pick from when the voice is new.
- **Text goes on in post.** Never ask an image or video model to write a price, a headline or a label.
- **Real products need the real photo** as a frame ref. Product-blind funnels show no product at all; check `OFFER.md`.
- **Never a real person.** No real faces, no real doctors, no celebrity or public-figure likeness or voice, no brand logos unless Ryan supplies the asset and asks.
- **Claims.** The video says only what the script says. Before-and-after pictures show what the script states and nothing more extreme.
- **Models change monthly.** Model ids and prices live in one place (`TALK_ENGINES`, `BROLL_ENGINES` in `tools/video/oneshot.py`, documented in `references/models.md`). When a new model ships, rerun the bake-off before changing a default.

## What it costs and how long it takes

Native voice on Seedance 2 mini is about 8 credits (4 cents) per second of script, B-roll about 16 credits a second, frames 10 credits each. The full 554-word Akka script (about 3.5 minutes, 10 B-roll clips, 11 new frames) estimates at 2,700 credits ($13.50), 3,500 with rerolls. `validate` prints the estimate and the balance for any plan. Wall time is mostly waiting on renders: 20 to 40 minutes.

## Known limits (say these when they apply)

- Talking shots render at 720p and are scaled to 1080x1920. Wan 2.7 ignores the audio at 1080p (0 of 3 takes), so 1080p talking is off the table until a model passes the bake-off there.
- Two faces talking in one shot is not supported. Dialogue is shot in singles, the way film covers a scene.
- Faces the scorer cannot find (objects with faces, masks, profiles) come back `NO_FACE` and are judged by eye only.
- Music: `music.file` mixes in a track Ryan supplies, or a Suno instrumental bed is generated on Kie (`ai-music-api/generate`, about 12 credits for 100 s, sat at -26 dB under the voice on Two Wrinkles). Music taste is Ryan's call; the builder cannot hear it.
- You cannot hear the result. The listening judge agreed with Ryan on the one voice he has graded and is wrong some of the time; voice casting needs his ear.
- Native voices are generated per line. A line can come back with a word changed or clipped; the word check catches it and rerolls, and a line that fails three times is reported, not hidden.
- Full-length native builds proven: TPS Two Wrinkles (2026-10-15/16, 1:38, voice-only format, seven versions shipped as VID828 to VID834). A full-length native build with a face talking on camera (yapper, drama) has not been run yet; the longest to-camera native test is two lines. Say so until one has.
- Transcribers (whisper, Gemini) cannot hear a doubled word or a mispronounced word. Strict word checks need `voice.second_ear` (Gemini repeat check) and, for any word Ryan or a client has flagged by ear, `voice.respell` plus `voice.phoneme_gate` (see plan-format.md). Pronunciation hints in the prompt do nothing on Seedance 2 mini.
