## Standing rules

- One brief covers everything. Choosing video should open the video questions inside the same brief, not spawn a second brief.
- No em dashes anywhere.
- VSL offers stay product-blind: no brand, bottle, price, guarantee, or benefit bullets. Unaware, problem aware, or solution aware only.

## What Ryan does NOT want in a brief (graded 2026-08-19, RJV/TPS/TFC yapper session)

His words: "I don't need any of the special notes. I don't know why you keep doing those special notes. We don't need them. We don't need image directions since this is a video. I don't need the proof ever. I also don't need the body script. That's not for me to put together. That's Fernando's job."

- **Field 10, Proof: cut. Always.** Do not argue for the test inside the brief. If a receipt matters, it belongs in field 11 Reference as an artifact, not as a case.
- **Field 14, Image direction: cut whenever the format is video.** Field 15 Video visual direction covers what is on screen. Image direction stays only for static formats.
- **Field 16, Special notes: cut.** The product-blind rules, the no-em-dash rule and the compliance lines are standing rules of this pack, not per-brief reminders.
- **No body script in a brief.** Ryan briefs, Fernando writes the script. Deliver hooks, not scripts, unless he asks for the copy itself.
- Do not add closing sections that argue for the idea ("why this is worth a slot", "why this account needs it"). The brief specifies the ad, it does not sell it.

**When a reference video exists, the brief shrinks (PROVISIONAL, from the 2026-08-26 TFC makeup-artist session).** If the batch is built on a reference ad, the concept is one short paragraph: "the reference with this swap" plus the story in 3-4 plain sentences and the mechanism in one. Visual notes are 1-2 sentences: "copy the reference video, here is the only thing that changes." No beat-by-beat retelling, no shot lists, no method labels (no "authority open", "betrayal stat", "product-blind", "substantiation") — the video carries the detail, the brief carries the swap and the guardrails.

**A yapper's activity needs a reason inside the story.** Ryan, same session, on a script where she chopped vegetables: "why is she chopping onions every night?" Hands busy is good and untested in most of our accounts, but the activity has to be something she is doing because of the story (sorting a box of photos that just arrived, emptying the shelf she is talking about), never set dressing chosen because it looks homely.
