# Strategist Advertorial for product-page funnels

You write the whole page in text: a story (or a doctor's lesson) that sells nothing for the first half, then a full sales page from the moment the product is named. The reader arrives from a cold ad and leaves by a button into the client's product page. There is no video to pay anything off. Everything the VSL would have done, this page does.

Ryan owns the page design and the images. Deliver plain text, section-divided, with placement notes.

## HARD RULE: structure only, never their copy (Ryan, 2026-10-15)

The market pages in the structure study taught the SKELETON: block order, where the product enters, where the first button sits, what the second half contains, how long each part runs. That is all they are for. Never reuse a headline, subhead, line, phrase, witness beat, comment, or close from any studied page, and never paraphrase one closely enough that it reads as theirs. Every word on our page comes from the house: `strategist-references/ryan-voice.md`, `hook-laws.md`, the ADVERTORIAL FORMAT RULES and HEADLINE FORMULAS in `swipe-learnings.md`, the offer's research pack, and her real lines from `data/VOICE-DIGEST.md`. Before delivering, grep the draft against `offers/<slug>/research/data/adv-study/txt-*.txt` and the client's `inputs/*.txt` for any shared sentence; if one turns up, rewrite it.

## STEP ZERO: ideas before drafts (Ryan's rule, every page)

Never write the page until Ryan has picked the shape and the headline. Pitch three concepts in one message, each as: headline, the opening scene in two lines, the mechanism door (the one sentence the friend or the doctor says), where the product enters, why it wins. He locks one. Then write.

The shapes, in the order his mind goes for a no-VSL offer:
1. **First-person story** (the market's winner: client pages and Hey Bliss). 4,000 to 5,500 words.
2. **Doctor's or insider's letter** (Miami MD "What Most Dermatologists Get Wrong"). 2,500 to 4,000 words. A lesson with a chart, four steps taught generically, a confession, then the product.
3. **Reasons listicle** (MyDermaDream "7 Reasons"). 1,200 to 2,000 words. Product named at the top, comparison table above the fold. Product-aware traffic only.
4. **Editorial review** (Granny Blog "we tested it 30 days"). 2,000 words. Needs a real feature or a real test to borrow; do not invent press.

## The story skeleton (18 blocks, in order, with where they sit)

Percentages are of the article, comments and footer excluded. Target 4,000 to 5,500 words of article.

| # | Block | Where | What it has to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Masthead, "Advertorial" label, byline, date, read time | top | A woman's name. Nobody pretends it is news. |
| 2 | Headline + one deck line | top | One sentence, written under hook-laws and the house HEADLINE FORMULAS (credibility opens, one quoted word, one caps word max, a number, a parenthetical). The structural shape that wins here: the authority low point or a stranger's reaction, then the turn. Write ours; never borrow a studied headline. |
| 3 | Mirror open | 0 to 3% | A mirror line or a scene. She is in the page inside 100 words. Ours, in Ryan's register. |
| 4 | The before, with receipts | 3 to 15% | Every product she bought with its price. The shame scenes. Her words from VOICE-DIGEST, not invented scenes she has to be in. |
| 5 | The authority low point | 15 to 20% | The dermatologist's five words, the surgeon's quote in the glove box. She gives up. |
| 6 | The friend with the face | 20 to 30% | A skeptical witness (a retired nurse, a roommate's photo, two photos a year apart). One sentence that contradicts everything she bought. This is where the mechanism is TEASED, never taught. |
| 7 | The order | 30 to 33% | Skeptical, late at night, the guarantee mentioned as the reason she risked it. Price logic planted, not sold. |
| 8 | The diary with witnesses | 33 to 48% | Morning one, day 3, weeks 1 to 6, months 2 to 4. One witness per beat, escalating: husband, daughter, neighbor, hairdresser, stranger, cashier, sister, the doctor who says "I was wrong." This is the proof. |
| 9 | The product named | 48 to 50% | A subhead that says the reader has been asking for the name (write our own). Name, one sentence of what it is, the routine, what it is not. |
| 10 | Why nothing worked and what is different | 50 to 62% | The mechanism, in the fourth-grade version. Then the ingredient cards: four, each with a job and one number, the star first. The comparison table (vs the needle, vs ordinary creams, vs the powder). A formulator or doctor quote if the client allows it. |
| 11 | When will you see results | 62 to 66% | Day one, week one, month one, month three. One line each. |
| 12 | Reviews | 66 to 72% | Three to six. Title, name, age, "Verified Buyer." Scenes with a witness, 40 to 80 words each. |
| 13 | Where to get it | 72 to 80% | Not in stores. The discount with the number. The bundles, the 3-jar as "what I wish I'd ordered the first time." Free shipping. The subscription truth (see client rules). The guarantee restated. Scarcity with a reason. **FIRST BUTTON HERE.** |
| 14 | Two paths | 80 to 85% | Path 1 leave, Path 2 try it risk-free. Button. |
| 15 | One last thing | 85 to 90% | The emotional P.S. "Not 'good for my age.'" The pool. Button. |
| 16 | Counterfeit warning + offer box + rating | 90 to 95% | "The only place to get the genuine formula." INTERNET ONLY OFFER. The rating line. Button. |
| 17 | Comments | after | 8 to 12. A doubter, a shipping question answered, a tag-a-friend pair, a long skeptic-turned-fan, a "not fair, I paid full price." House rule: 15 to 30 words each, name the thing, one big win, one reply with a link. |
| 18 | Footer | after | Advertorial notice, fictional-persons line where the client requires it, results vary, citations. |

**Button law.** No button before block 13. Before that the product name is a word in the story, never a link. From block 13 on, one button every 150 to 300 words. One button text, every time (the client's is CHECK AVAILABILITY NOW). The headline, the deck and the first screen are where the page is won or lost (SCR Clarity: the leak is the first 10% of scroll), so the first screen gets the most drafts.

## The laws that carry over from the VSL advertorial (none are optional)

- **Skim test.** Headline plus subheads alone carry the whole argument, in order, no gaps. Every referent defined by the headline or an earlier subhead. Read them aloud as a sequence before delivering.
- **One succinct subhead per section.** Never stacks.
- **Mobile-first.** One idea per line. No paragraph over three phone lines.
- **Phenomenon, not assumed scenes.** The diary is HER story; the reader is never told what she did last Tuesday.
- **Specific and visual.** Prices on every enemy. Names on every witness. Numbers on every ingredient (ingredient-level only when the client says so).
- **Named mechanism grammar.** [the thing she owns] + [a machine word]. The mechanism is taught in the fourth-grade version first, the jargon inside the body.
- **Ryan's sentence rules.** Natural spoken sentences, no staccato stacks as the default, no "This isn't X, it's Y," no three-part lists, no colons that spend the reveal. No em dashes anywhere.
- **Iterate surgically.** When Ryan grades, keep every ungraded line word for word.
- **Compliance.** The offer's client rules carried through every block (for Olavita: never Botox affiliation or superiority, "visible cosmetic appearance" only; no "cure"; never name Miami MD or Gundry MD; 65% no code; ingredient-level proof only; testimonials are written and disclosed as such; "made in Korea" is unresolved, do not lean on it).

## What is different from the VSL advertorial (the part this skill exists for)

- **The reveal is spent on the page.** The product is named at the halfway point, under its own subhead. The VSL skill holds it for the click; this skill does not.
- **The second half is a sales page.** Mechanism, ingredients, comparison, timeline, reviews, offer, guarantee, scarcity, two paths, P.S., counterfeit, comments. Every block in the table above exists in the winners. Skipping one is a choice to argue for, not a default.
- **The offer facts come from the product page, verified.** Price, bundles, discount, shipping, guarantee terms, subscription terms. If the product page contradicts itself (Olavita shows two prices for the 3-jar bundle), the page states what the client confirmed, never a guess. Open asks go in the deliverable header.
- **The button goes to the product page the client names.** Never to a cart URL guessed from the HTML.
- **Comments are written with the page**, not added later, and they answer the three questions every reader has at the bottom: does it work, how long does shipping take, is it real.

## Graded in 2026-10-15 (first Olavita page, v1 to v2)

- **The open is never credentials.** "I spent 22 years at a skin clinic" as the first line was graded dead on arrival. The page opens the way Ryan's own story landers open: one claim line with the point in the first five words, then a qualifier line ("If you are a woman over 60 with..."), then a real lead of 150 to 250 words before subhead 1. Credentials arrive inside the lead as a fact that raises the stakes, never as a warm-up.
- **An image after every subhead, two where the section runs long.** The house controls carry 26 to 32 images on 9 to 11 subheads. Every image note is literal and phone-real (the drawer of jars, the calendar with four circled dates, the FaceTime screenshot, the before/after with the black bar). "Ryan's call" is not an image note.
- **Body in the lander register.** One idea per line, bullets where she lists things, a bold mini-head or a bold quote inside long sections, whole sentences not fragments.
- **Read the model copy before writing**, not only the rules: `offers/mofo/advertorials/advertorial-sexdrive-copy.md` and Ryan's "Personal Story LF Lander" in `strategist-references/source/ryan-copy-corpus/FULL-CORPUS.md` (grep the title).
- **Subheads** (nine spine rounds): say the biggest universal pain flat in her words; act as a headline for the next 300 words with one tease; scenes are body copy; handle the scary ingredient inside the story before she orders; check the section count against the market (client pages 17 to 20).
